Orbital Reconstruction from Angular Astrometry

Tracking Near-Earth Asteroids (NEAs) is fundamental to planetary defense and hazard assessment. When an asteroid is faint and rapidly drifting across the sky, astronomers only record 2D topocentric angular coordinates (right ascension $\alpha$ and declination $\delta$) from a telescope, with no direct distance measurement. How accurately can one reconstruct the full six Keplerian orbital elements of the body around the Sun using only three optical snapshots taken over 26 days?

The Method of Gauss and Iterative Orbit Determination

We acquired and reduced raw FITS charge-coupled device (CCD) images using Las Cumbres Observatory (LCO) telescopes located in Chile, Hawaii, and Texas. After performing astrometric calibration against 20 reference stars per exposure to establish plate constants, we implemented the Method of Gauss (1801) for initial orbit determination in Python.

Because the asteroid's heliocentric position vectors $\mathbf{r}_1, \mathbf{r}_2, \mathbf{r}_3$ lie in a single orbital plane, the middle vector can be expressed as a linear combination of the outer two: $\mathbf{r}_2 = a_1 \mathbf{r}_1 + a_3 \mathbf{r}_3$. Using Kepler's second law (conservation of areal velocity), the triangular areas ratio is iteratively refined with fourth-order Taylor series in Gaussian time intervals $\tau = k(t - t_2)$ until convergence to $10^{-10}\text{ AU}$. Random observational centroid noise was propagated through 1,000 Monte Carlo trials.

Iterative 4th-Order Gauss $f$ and $g$ Series
$$\mathbf{r}(\tau) = f(\tau)\,\mathbf{r}_2 + g(\tau)\,\dot{\mathbf{r}}_2$$ $$f(\tau) = 1 - \frac{\mu}{2r_2^3}\tau^2 + \dots, \qquad g(\tau) = \tau - \frac{\mu}{6r_2^3}\tau^3 + \dots$$
Symbol Key: $\tau = k(t - t_2)$ is the Gaussian time interval ($k$ is the Gaussian gravitational constant), $\mu = 1$ in canonical solar units, and $r_2 = \|\mathbf{r}_2\|$ is the asteroid's heliocentric distance at the second observation.

Geometric Conditioning and Observational Uncertainty

The Monte Carlo uncertainty resulting from astrometric centroid noise was tiny (for example, $\pm 0.005\text{ AU}$ in the semi-major axis)—far smaller than the gap to NASA JPL Horizons. This discrepancy demonstrates an essential physical principle: high observational precision does not guarantee accuracy.

The errors are systematic: the classical Method of Gauss assumes an idealized two-body Keplerian problem, neglecting planetary gravitational perturbations from Jupiter and Earth. Furthermore, because asteroid 2015 GF27 has a shallow inclination ($i \approx 10^\circ$), the out-of-plane signal is small, which mathematically amplifies the sensitivity of the node and periapsis to minor observational errors. The mean anomaly error then compounds from the slight offset in semi-major axis through Kepler's equation.

The 45-Year Controversy: Fast vs. Slow Injection

Are neutron stars born spinning fast, or are many born slow? Standard core-collapse supernova theory predicts that newly formed neutron stars should be born spinning rapidly, with periods of tens of milliseconds ($P \sim 0.01 - 0.05\text{ s}$), due to conservation of angular momentum during stellar collapse. However, in 1981, Vivekanand and Narayan published an influential argument concluding that 40% to 75% of pulsars enter the population as "slow births" at periods of 0.40 to 0.85 s.

Settling this question from telescope catalogues has proved notoriously difficult for two reasons: a pulsar's spin-down deceleration scales with its magnetic field as $\dot{P} \propto B^2/P$ (meaning deceleration varies by six orders of magnitude across the population), and radio telescope surveys suffer from severe, poorly constrained observational selection effects.

Weak-Form Continuity Inversion in Phase Space

Pulsars do not jump discontinuously; they drift deterministically through the period-magnetic field $(P, B)$ plane. Consequently, their birth injection rate $S(P,B)$ must satisfy a two-dimensional continuity equation:

Weak-Form Phase-Space Continuity Inversion
$$\nabla \cdot (\mathbf{v}f) + \Lambda f = S$$ $$\iint \Big[ \mathbf{v}f \cdot \nabla\phi - \Lambda f \phi + S\phi \Big] \,d(\log P)\,d(\log B) = 0$$
Symbol Key: $\mathbf{v} = (\dot{P}, \dot{B})$ is the deterministic kinematic drift vector, $f(P,B)$ is the true phase-space density, $\Lambda$ is the death/luminosity cutoff rate, $S$ is the birth distribution to be recovered, and $\phi$ is an infinitely differentiable test function.

Taking numerical derivatives of noisy, bin-counted observational histograms creates catastrophic numerical divergence. We resolved this by formulating the continuity equation in weak form: multiplying by smooth Sobolev test functions $\phi$ and integrating by parts transfers all spatial derivatives off the noisy catalogue data and onto the smooth test function. Crucially, telescope visibility selection was calibrated strictly outside the contested slow-birth window ($0.40 - 0.85\text{ s}$), eliminating any possibility of circular reasoning.

Demographic Reconstruction and Catalog Alignment

Applied to 2,356 isolated pulsars from the ATNF catalogue, the inversion demonstrates that only 0.57% of pulsars are born slow (with a formal upper bound of at most 9.03% across all physical crustal field-decay timescales). The classical 40%–75% slow-birth hypothesis is decisively excluded. Four independent physical tests that do not depend on the fitted visibility function confirm this conclusion:

  • Supernova Remnant Census: Associating young pulsars directly with known supernova remnants constrains slow births to $\le 22\%$.
  • Scale-Height Kinematic Clock: Measuring vertical drift away from the Galactic plane over time bounds slow births to $\le 17\%$ ($2.6\sigma$ tension with the 50% slow-birth hypothesis).
  • Volume-Complete Solar Cylinders: A local neighborhood census ($d \le 1.5\text{ kpc}$) bounds slow births to $4\% - 9\%$.
  • High-Field Conservation Bound: Restricts slow births to $\le 23\%$.

Astrophysical Implications for Neutron Star Birth

This paper settles a four-decade debate in compact-object astrophysics. It proves that the observed accumulation of slow pulsars is an optical illusion caused by rapid deceleration rather than slow injection, establishing that core-collapse supernovae almost universally produce rapidly rotating neutron stars.

Technical Notes: Synthetic Validation & Resolution Boundaries

Across 100 synthetic catalogues of 2,356 pulsars with known fast, Gaussian, and bimodal injection profiles, the weak-form inversion recovered the underlying distribution to within 0.06 dex in $\log P$, with an effective resolution of 0.29 dex inside the slow window. The paper also documents its resolution limit: slow-birth fractions below 25%–30% fall below current observational sensitivity and require next-generation instruments (such as the Square Kilometre Array) to constrain further.

The 0.6-Second Peak and Residence-Time Slowdown

Paper I proves that pulsars are born spinning rapidly, with birth injection peaking near 48 ms. Why, then, does the observed astronomical catalogue peak near 0.6 s? The timed catalogue of 2,356 pulsars represents roughly 3% of the active Galaxy. What is the true, intrinsic demographic distribution of the entire Milky Way, and what does the unseen population look like?

Phase-Space Transport and Kinematic Selection Modeling

The apparent contradiction is resolved by residence time (the "traffic jam" effect of stellar spin-down). A pulsar's residence time per logarithmic period decade scales as $\tau_{\rm res} \propto P^2/B^2$.

A newborn pulsar flashes across the fast regime in roughly 270 years, but takes approximately 23 million years to traverse the mature period range of 0.5 to 3 s—a slowdown factor of $\mathbf{10^5}$. Consequently, pulsars spend roughly 71% of their active radiating lifetimes lingering at slower periods. We evolved the birth distribution forward in time under magnetic dipole braking, crustal ohmic field decay, and death-valley switchoff, solving the 2D steady-state transport equation analytically and validating it against an independent 100,000-star Fortran Monte Carlo simulation evolved over 100 Myr ($W_1 = 0.028\text{ dex}$ agreement).

Steady-State Demographic Transport
$$\partial_x(v_x f) + \partial_y(v_y f) + \Lambda f = S$$ $$v_x \propto B^2 P^{-(n-1)}, \qquad \tau_{\rm res} \propto \frac{P^2}{B^2}$$
Symbol Key: $x = \log P$, $y = \log B$, $v_x$ is magnetic dipole braking drift, $v_y$ is crustal ohmic decay, and $\tau_{\rm res}$ is the residence timescale per decade of spin period.

The True Galactic Census and Scale-Height Invariants

  • True Population Peak: The actual Galactic population peaks at $1.61\text{ s}$ (median $1.38\text{ s}$), reproducing the observed $0.62\text{ s}$ catalogue peak once telescope beaming and luminosity thresholds are applied ($W_1 = 0.037\text{ dex}$). No slow injection is required.
  • Milky Way Inventory: The Galaxy contains $(0.82 \pm 0.27) \times 10^5$ active radio pulsars and roughly $1.1 \times 10^8$ dead remnants.
  • Blind Kinematic Test: Ballistic drift away from the Galactic disc correctly predicts high-latitude pulsars without parameter tuning ($W_1 = 0.031\text{ dex}$, Kolmogorov-Smirnov $p = 0.199$).
  • Survey Forecasts: Predicts discovery medians will shift to $0.83\text{ s}$ for FAST and $1.03\text{ s}$ for SKA1-Mid.

Cyclewise Digit-Subtraction Dynamics and Single-Generator Systems

Iterated digit-rearrangement-and-subtraction maps originated with D.R. Kaprekar's 1949 observation that sorting digits descending and ascending and subtracting drives every non-repdigit 4-digit number to 6174 within 7 steps. Subsequent generalizations either analyzed sorting routines across bases (Lapenta, Ludington, Prichett; Yamagami, Matsui; Kay, Downes-Ward) or studied global permutations $|P_1(x) - P_2(x)|$ (Smarandache).

This paper introduces a fundamentally different arithmetic dynamical system $F_\pi$ defined on $k$-digit base-$B$ numerals for a fixed permutation $\pi \in S_k$ with disjoint cycle decomposition $\pi = \pi_1 \pi_2 \cdots \pi_m$. For each cycle $\pi_i = (a_1 a_2 \cdots a_t)$ in canonical form:

  1. Read: Form the local $t$-digit base-$B$ number $x_i$ from positions $a_1, \dots, a_t$.
  2. Rotate: Compute $\rho_t(x_i)$, the cyclic right-rotation of $x_i$.
  3. Subtract: Compute the local difference $g_i = |x_i - \rho_t(x_i)|$, retaining leading zeros.
  4. Scatter: Write the resulting digits back to the original cycle positions $a_1, \dots, a_t$.

Because the transformation is governed by a single generator $\pi$, its cycle partition is static under iteration, preserving coordinate alignment across all steps and enabling exact decoupling.

Direct-Product Decomposition & Linear Semiconjugacy

The global map $F_\pi$ decouples into an exact direct product of independent local maps: $F_\pi \cong g_{t_1} \times \cdots \times g_{t_m}$. The global period of any digit vector $d$ is the least common multiple of its local cycle periods: $\ell(d) = \mathrm{lcm}(\ell_1, \dots, \ell_m)$. Furthermore, the divisibility invariant $(B - 1) \mid F_\pi(x)$ holds universally for all states.

Each local map $g_t$ satisfies complementation symmetry $g_t(B^t - 1 - x) = g_t(x)$ and linearizes on the full modulus $N = B^t - 1$:

Linearization on Signed Classes & Periodic Bijection
$$g_t(v) \equiv \pm c v \pmod{B^t - 1}, \quad \text{where } c = (B - 1)B^{t-1}$$ $$\kappa_N(g_t(v)) = \kappa_N(c \cdot \kappa_N(v)), \quad \kappa_N(v) = \min(v, N - v)$$
Semiconjugacy to Linear Model: The canonical representative map $\kappa_N$ is a semiconjugacy from $g_t$ to multiplication by $c$ on $\pm$-classes modulo $N$. Crucially, $\kappa_N$ restricts to a strict bijection between the periodic points of $g_t$ and the periodic $\pm$-classes under multiplication by $c$.

The Coprime Splitting, Attractor Rank & Orbit Periods

Factoring $N = B^t - 1 = N_1 N_2$ uniquely such that every prime divisor of $N_1$ divides $B - 1$ and $\gcd(N_2, B - 1) = 1$ completely separates the transient from the periodic dynamics:

Attractor Cardinality, Orbit LCM & Eventual Rank
$$|\mathrm{Per}(g_t)| = \frac{N_2(B, t) + 1}{2}, \qquad |\mathrm{Im}(F_\pi^j)| = \prod_{i=1}^m \frac{N_2(B, t_i) + 1}{2} \quad (j \ge \max_i B^{t_i})$$ $$P_* = \mathrm{ord}_{(\mathbb{Z}/N_2\mathbb{Z})^\times / \{\pm 1\}}(c) = \min\{j \ge 1 : c^j \equiv \pm 1 \pmod{N_2}\}$$
Periodic Structure: Since $N_2$ is always odd, periodic $\pm$-classes are precisely the multiples of $N_1$, giving exactly $(N_2 + 1)/2$ periodic attractor states. For class $N_1 r$, the minimal period is $P(r) = \min\{j \ge 1 : c^j \equiv \pm 1 \pmod{N_2 / \gcd(r, N_2)}\}$, with $P_*$ being the LCM of all periodic-orbit lengths.

Universal Theorems & Dynamical Invariants

  • Exact Fixed-Point Count: For $B \ge 2$ and $t \ge 2$, the number of fixed points of $g_t$ is exactly $1 + \lfloor \frac{(B - 1)\gcd(B^{t-1} - 2, 2B - 1)}{2B - 1} \rfloor$.
  • Sharp Two-Value Transient Bound: Let $a_0$ be the least integer $\ge 0$ such that $N_1 \mid c^{a_0}$. The exact transient length $\iota$ to reach $\mathrm{Per}(g_t)$ satisfies $a_0 \le \iota \le a_0 + 1$. Every state is periodic within $a_0 + 1$ iterations.
  • Two-Cycle Spread Map & OEIS A003602: For $t = 2$, local dynamics reduce to the spread map $\beta(m) = |2m - (B + 1)|$. Writing $B + 1 = 2^s u_0$ ($u_0$ odd), every starting spread reaches the invariant set $E(B)$ in $\le s + 1$ steps (sharp). The eventual rank is $(u_0 + 1)/2 = a(B + 1)$, proving an exact correspondence with Kimberling's paraphrase of the binary system (OEIS A003602).

  • Single vs. Multi-Generator Dynamics: Composing maps with different permutations breaks cycle partition alignment, causing the coordinatewise product decomposition to vanish and highlighting the unique structural tractability of single-generator systems.

Closed-Form Convergence and Central Binomial Sums

While manipulating series expansions for $\pi$ by hand, an algebraic error produced the unexpected sum: $$\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{2^n}{\binom{2n+1}{n+1}} = 1+\frac{\pi}{2}$$ Can this be rigorously proven without relying on generalized hypergeometric machinery (${}_2F_1$), and generalized into a continuous family of rapidly converging formulas?

Elementary Calculus via the Beta-Integral Representation

We construct a self-contained derivation for the entire one-parameter family $S(a) = \sum_{n \ge 0} \frac{a^n}{\binom{2n+1}{n+1}}$ valid for all real $|a| < 4$. Rather than invoking heavy hypergeometric identities, the proof applies Euler's Beta integral identity to rewrite the reciprocal central binomial coefficient: $$\frac{1}{\binom{2n+1}{n+1}} = (n+1)\int_0^1 t^n(1-t)^n\,dt$$ Because $t(1-t) \le 1/4$ on $[0,1]$, the power series converges uniformly for $|a| < 4$. Interchanging summation and integration converts the sum into $\int_0^1 \frac{dt}{(1 - at(1-t))^2}$, which is evaluated directly by completing the square and applying elementary substitutions.

Closed-Form Evaluation for Real $|a| < 4$
$$S(a) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{a^n}{\binom{2n+1}{n+1}}$$ $$= \frac{2}{4-a} + \frac{8}{(4-a)^{3/2}\sqrt{a}}\,\arctan\sqrt{\frac{a}{4-a}}$$
Special Exact Values: $S(1) = \frac{2}{3} + \frac{4\pi}{9\sqrt{3}}$, $S(2) = 1 + \frac{\pi}{2}$, and $S(3) = 2 + \frac{8\pi}{3\sqrt{3}}$.

Geometric Convergence and Special Harmonic Evaluations

The terms shrink geometrically at rate $(a/4)^n$. In contrast to the classical Leibniz alternating series which requires $10^7$ terms for 7 decimal digits of $\pi$, choosing the acceleration parameter $a = 2 - \sqrt{2}$ yields 7 correct digits of $\pi$ in just 8 terms.

1. Submultiplicative Sequences & Fekete’s Subadditive Lemma

Given a finite generating code $S \subset \{0, 1\}^*$ of cardinality $|S| = k \ge 2$, let $S^{\le n}$ denote the set of words formed by concatenating at most $n$ codewords from $S$. Concatenating any word in $S^{\le m}$ with a word in $S^{\le n}$ yields a word in $S^{\le (m+n)}$. Consequently, the concatenation map $S^{\le m} \times S^{\le n} \to S^{\le (m+n)}$ is surjective onto its image, establishing submultiplicativity:

Submultiplicativity & Asymptotic Effectiveness
$$a_{m+n} \le a_m a_n \quad \text{for } a_n = |S^{\le n}|$$ $$\mathrm{Eff}(S) = \lim_{n\to\infty} a_n^{1/n} = \inf_{n\ge 1} a_n^{1/n}, \qquad 1 \le \mathrm{Eff}(S) \le |S|$$
Proof Mechanism: Taking logarithms transforms $a_{m+n} \le a_m a_n$ into $b_{m+n} \le b_m + b_n$ for $b_n = \ln a_n$. Applying Fekete’s Subadditive Lemma guarantees that $\lim_{n\to\infty} b_n/n = \inf_{n\ge 1}(b_n/n)$ exists finitely. Exponentiating yields $\mathrm{Eff}(S)$. The Compositional System: Modular Serialism & Interval Equivalence

The quartet adapts the principles of musical serialism by deriving its pitch material from number-theoretic sequences, but departs from classical twelve-tone technique in two foundational ways:

  • Relative intervals rather than absolute pitches: In traditional twelve-tone serialism (Schoenberg, Webern), the composer defines a rigid row of fixed pitch classes ($P_0, P_1, \dots, P_{11}$). In this system, each sequence term $x_n$ dictates the interval distance to leap from the current note, leaving absolute pitch, register, and contour flexible.
  • Deterministic integer sequences rather than tone rows: The interval distances are drawn from sequences such as the Fibonacci numbers ($1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, \dots$), primes, or polynomial sequences, whose recurrent mathematical properties mirror organic growth.
1. Mathematical Foundations: The Three Equivalence Axioms

To project an unbounded integer sequence into finite musical space, each term is reduced modulo an integer $m$:

$$a \equiv x_n \pmod m \qquad \text{where } a \in \{0, 1, \dots, m-1\}$$

The residue $a$ is then governed by three algebraic symmetries that define the composer's palette:

  • Axiom I: Directional Invariance ($+a \equiv -a$): Stepping upward by $a$ scale steps and stepping downward by $a$ scale steps are defined as acoustic manifestations of the identical interval class. The number specifies the magnitude of the displacement; direction (up or down) is a free expressive choice.

    Why Serialist Inversions Cannot Exist: In classical twelve-tone serialism, an "Inversion" creates a new row form by reflecting every ascending interval into a descending one ($+a \mapsto -a$). In this system, because $+a \equiv -a$, reflecting the sign merely maps the interval back onto itself: $$\mathcal{I}(+a) = -a \equiv +a$$ Therefore, an inversion produces no new row form—the system is inherently self-inverse by construction. The classical Prime ($P$) and Inversion ($I$) operations collapse into an exact algebraic identity.
  • Axiom II: Complementary Equivalence ($+a \equiv -(m - a)$): On a circular pitch scale of $m$ notes, stepping forward by $a$ arrives at the exact same pitch degree as stepping backward by the modular complement $m - a$: $$+a \equiv -(m - a) \pmod m \qquad \text{and} \qquad -a \equiv +(m - a) \pmod m$$ Because direction is invariant ($+a \equiv -a$), all four physical motions: $$\mathcal{E}(a) = \bigl\{ +a, \quad -a, \quad +(m - a), \quad -(m - a) \bigr\}$$ belong to the exact same interval class. Whether the composer moves up by $a$, down by $a$, up by $m - a$, or down by $m - a$, they are realizing different facets of the same mathematical constraint.
  • Axiom III: Octave Equivalence ($a \equiv a + m$): Adding or subtracting integer multiples of the modulus $m$ corresponds to transposing the interval across octaves ($a \equiv a + k \cdot m$). A stepwise second ($a = 1$) can be voiced across octaves as a compound ninth ($1 + 7 = 8$) or sixteenth, allowing the quartet to move between close contrapuntal weaving and wide registral leaps.
2. Modulus & Pitch Space Partitioning

The choice of modulus $m$ establishes the harmonic geometry:

  • Modulo 7 ($m = 7$ — Diatonic E Natural Minor): Operates on the seven scale degrees of the E natural minor (Aeolian) scale:
    $$1 = \text{E}, \quad 2 = \text{F}\sharp, \quad 3 = \text{G}, \quad 4 = \text{A}, \quad 5 = \text{B}, \quad 6 = \text{C}, \quad 7 = \text{D}$$
    Under the equivalence axioms, all integers in the universe partition into just four fundamental interval classes:
Interval Class Primary Residue $a$ Complement $m - a$ Equivalence Set $\mathcal{E}(a)$ Musical Function in E Minor
Class 0 $0$ $0$ $\{0\}$ Unison, repeated notes, stationary pedal points
Class 1 $1$ $6$ $\{+1, -1, +6, -6\}$ Seconds and sevenths (scalar, stepwise melodic voice-leading)
Class 2 $2$ $5$ $\{+2, -2, +5, -5\}$ Thirds and sixths (triadic harmony: E–G, G–B, C–E)
Class 3 $3$ $4$ $\{+3, -3, +4, -4\}$ Fourths and fifths (open structural consonances: E–B, A–E)
  • Modulo 11 ($m = 11$ — Chromatic Pitch Space): Partitions the chromatic scale into eleven interval classes, generating dense, post-tonal counterpoint and restless harmonic friction.
3. Worked-Out Musical Trace: How E Minor Harmony Emerges

To see why this produces coherent musical architecture rather than random leaps, consider the first six Fibonacci terms ($1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8$) reduced modulo 7, starting on the tonic note E4:

Notice what just happened: through pure mathematical deduction, the Fibonacci sequence naturally spelled out an ascending E minor arpeggio (E4 → F♯4 → G4 → B4 → E5) followed by a balanced scalar return! The interplay between the Pisano period and the interval equivalence classes naturally generates organic, classical harmonic balance.

4. Polyphony & Composer Agency Across the Quartet

The mathematical sequence never composes the piece alone; it provides the architectural scaffolding:

  • Contrapuntal distribution: The sequence is allocated across four distinct acoustic voices (Violin I, Violin II, Viola, Violoncello). While Violin I carries an ascending realization $+a$, the Cello can ground the texture with the complementary descent $-(m-a)$ or an octave displacement $a + 7$, and the inner voices supply harmonic counter-lines.
  • Rhythm, meter, and pacing: Intervals specify distance, not duration. The composer assigns note values (sustained whole notes, syncopated quarters, rapid sixteenths), meters (4/4, 3/4), and tempos.
  • Timbre and articulation: Dynamics from pianissimo to fortissimo, expressive vibrato, sul ponticello, and pizzicato are purely expressive decisions.
5. The Deliberate Exception at Measure 27

A purely mechanical algorithmic process would cycle through its sequence indefinitely without ever reaching musical closure. Throughout the opening Andante, the Fibonacci intervals drive escalating rhythmic and contrapuntal compression.

At measure 27, the quartet intentionally breaks the mathematical sequence. The strict interval row is suspended to voice an authentic classical cadence, releasing the accumulated contrapuntal tension and inaugurating the serene, chorale-like Adagio in E minor.
