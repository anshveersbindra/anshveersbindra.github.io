Astrophysics · Computational Fluid Dynamics With Tanish Kulkarni · 2026 · Paper I of Pulsar Series

A Weak-Form 2D Continuity Inversion for Pulsar Birth Demographics

⚡

The 10-Second Takeaway: For 45 years, astronomers debated whether neutron stars are born spinning blindingly fast or sluggishly slow. By treating 2,356 drifting pulsars as a fluid and solving the inverse continuity equation in "weak form," we prove that neutron stars are overwhelmingly born spinning fast, settling a classic 1981 controversy.

1. The Question & The Paradox

Do neutron stars begin their lives spinning tens of times per second (tens of milliseconds), or are up to 75% injected at slow periods of $0.40$ to $0.85\text{ s}$? In 1981, Vivekanand and Narayan deduced that most pulsars are born slow. But counting pulsars directly from telescope data is deceptively hard: pulsar braking scales with magnetic field as $\dot{P} \propto B^2/P$ (varying by six orders of magnitude across the population), and radio surveys suffer from severe, unknown observational selection effects.

2. The Method & Mathematical Intuition

Pulsars do not jump randomly; they drift deterministically through the period-magnetic field $(P, B)$ plane. Thus, their birth rate $S$ must obey a 2D continuity equation. Differentiating raw, noisy observational histograms directly amplifies noise into massive false signals. To solve this, we formulated the equation in weak form: integrating against smooth test functions transfers the derivatives off the noisy catalogue data and onto the infinitely smooth test functions. Crucially, telescope visibility was calibrated exclusively outside the contested slow-birth window ($0.40 - 0.85\text{ s}$), eliminating any possibility of circular reasoning.

Core Governing Equation · Weak Continuity Inversion Phase Space $(P, B)$
$$\nabla \cdot \big(\mathbf{v}\,f\big) + \Lambda f = S \quad \implies \quad \iint \Big[ \mathbf{v}f \cdot \nabla\phi - \Lambda f \phi + S\phi \Big] \,d(\log P)\,d(\log B) = 0$$
Symbol Key: $\mathbf{v}$ is deterministic drift velocity $(\dot{P}, \dot{B})$, $f(P,B)$ is true pulsar phase-space density, $\Lambda$ is death/luminosity cutoff rate, $S(P,B)$ is the birth injection rate to be reconstructed, and $\phi$ is a smooth Sobolev test function that absorbs all spatial derivatives.
3. Concrete Results & Multi-Wavelength Validation

Applying this inversion to the complete ATNF catalogue of 2,356 isolated pulsars reveals that only $0.57\%$ of pulsars are born slow (with a formal upper limit of $\le 9.03\%$ across all field-decay timescales). The classical 40%–75% slow-birth hypothesis is ruled out. We verified this through four independent, calibration-free physical cross-checks:

  • Supernova Remnant Census: Constrains slow births to $\le 22\%$.
  • Galactic Scale-Height Clock: Kinematic drift away from the Galactic plane bounds slow births to $\le 17\%$ ($2.6\sigma$ tension with the $50\%$ hypothesis).
  • Volume-Complete Solar Cylinders: Local neighborhood census bounds slow births to $4\% - 9\%$.
  • High-Field Conservation Bound: Restricts slow births to $\le 23\%$.
4. Scientific Significance

This paper resolves a foundational debate in high-energy astrophysics, demonstrating that the apparent abundance of slow pulsars is an illusion caused by rapid spin-down rather than slow birth injection. It establishes that core-collapse supernovae almost universally produce rapidly rotating compact objects.

Technical Deep-Dive: Synthetic Benchmarks & Resolution Limits Click to Expand ↓

Monte Carlo Validation: Before running on real telescope data, we evaluated the inversion on 100 synthetic catalogues of 2,356 pulsars with known injection profiles (Fast, Gaussian, and Bimodal). The algorithm accurately recovered the birth distribution to within $0.06\text{ dex}$ in $\log P$, with an effective resolution of $0.29\text{ dex}$ inside the slow window.

Resolution Boundary: The paper explicitly documents its own physical threshold: slow-birth fractions below $25\% - 30\%$ fall beneath the observational noise floor of current radio telescopes and cannot be definitively ruled out until next-generation surveys (SKA, ngVLA) come online.

Astrophysics · Galactic Population Synthesis With Tanish Kulkarni · 2026 · Paper II of Pulsar Series (AASTeX631)

The Intrinsic Equilibrium Demographic Distribution of Galactic Pulsars

🌌

The 10-Second Takeaway: If pulsars are born spinning at 48 milliseconds, why do our telescope surveys peak at 0.6 seconds? We model the entire evolutionary life of Milky Way pulsars, showing that stars spend 100,000 times longer creeping through mature spin periods, explaining the apparent population without needing any slow births.

1. The Question: The Missing 97%

Paper I showed that pulsars are born fast, peaking near $48\text{ ms}$. But the observed catalog of 2,356 timed pulsars represents barely $3\%$ of the Galaxy's active population. What does the true, intrinsic demographic distribution of the entire Milky Way look like? And why does the observed catalog peak around $0.6\text{ s}$ if every pulsar started at $0.05\text{ s}$?

2. The Method & The Factor of $10^5$

The explanation lies in residence time. A pulsar's dwell time per decade of spin period scales as $\tau_{\rm res} \propto P^2/B^2$. A newborn pulsar flashes across the fast regime in just $\sim 270\text{ years}$, but takes over $\mathbf{23\text{ million years}}$ to cross the mature period range of $0.5$ to $3\text{ s}$—a residence slowdown factor of $\mathbf{10^5}$! Pulsars spend $71\%$ of their active radiating lives at slow periods. We solved the steady-state 2D continuity equation analytically in a single sweep and verified it against a custom 100,000-star Fortran Monte Carlo simulation over $100\text{ Myr}$.

Equilibrium Transport & Kinematic Velocity Steady-State Solution
$$\partial_x\big(v_x f\big) + \partial_y\big(v_y f\big) + \Lambda f = S, \quad \text{where } v_x \propto B^2 P^{-(n-1)}, \quad \tau_{\rm res} \propto \frac{P^2}{B^2}$$
Symbol Key: $x = \log P$, $y = \log B$, $v_x$ is magnetic dipole braking drift, $v_y$ is crustal ohmic field decay, and $\tau_{\rm res}$ is residence time per logarithmic period decade.
3. Concrete Demographics & Blind Tests
  • True Population Peak: The real Galactic population peaks at $1.61\text{ s}$ with a median of $1.38\text{ s}$, perfectly reproducing the observed $0.62\text{ s}$ telescope peak once radio beam geometry and luminosity thresholds are accounted for ($W_1 = 0.037\text{ dex}$).
  • Milky Way Inventory: The Galaxy hosts $(0.82 \pm 0.27) \times 10^5$ active radio pulsars and $\sim 1.1 \times 10^8$ cold dead remnants.
  • Blind Spatial Test: Ballistic drift out of the Galactic disk accurately predicts high-latitude pulsars without tuning ($W_1 = 0.031\text{ dex}$, KS test $p = 0.199$).
  • Survey Forecasts: Predicts discovery medians will shift to $0.83\text{ s}$ for FAST and $1.03\text{ s}$ for the Square Kilometre Array (SKA1-Mid).
4. Scientific Significance

Completes the two-paper series by providing an end-to-end, physically consistent demographic map of compact objects in the Milky Way, linking newborn supernova dynamics to the cold, dead pulsar graveyard of our Galaxy.

Observational Astronomy · Summer Science Program (SSP) With Chloe Leung & Gavin Flajole · LCO Telescopes (Chile, Hawaii, Texas)

Orbit Determination of Near-Earth Asteroid 2015 GF27 via the Method of Gauss

🚀

The 10-Second Takeaway: We tracked an elusive Near-Earth Asteroid using Las Cumbres Observatory telescopes across three continents, computed its full 3D Keplerian orbit from only three images, matched NASA JPL ephemerides to within 0.11%–0.61%, and uncovered a key lesson in real astronomy: why high precision is not the same as true accuracy.

1. The Question: Planetary Defense from 3 Snapshots

Near-Earth Asteroid (NEA) tracking is vital for planetary defense. But when a newly discovered asteroid is faint and fast-moving, can you reconstruct its complete six-parameter 3D elliptical orbit around the Sun using only three optical telescope observations spanning 26 days?

2. The Method: Gauss 1801 with 4th-Order Perturbations

We acquired and reduced raw FITS images from Las Cumbres Observatory (LCO) telescopes in Chile, Hawaii, and Texas, performing astrometry calibrated against 20 reference stars per frame. We wrote a Python engine implementing Gauss's 1801 method, expressing the middle position vector as a coplanar combination $\mathbf{r}_2 = a_1\mathbf{r}_1 + a_3\mathbf{r}_3$ and refining it iteratively with 4th-order series $\mathbf{r}(\tau) = f(\tau)\mathbf{r}_2 + g(\tau)\dot{\mathbf{r}}_2$ until convergence to $10^{-10}\text{ AU}$. Measurement uncertainties were propagated with Monte Carlo trials.

Gauss Orbit Iteration & 4th-Order $f$ and $g$ Series Heliocentric Position Refinement
$$\mathbf{r}(\tau) = f(\tau)\,\mathbf{r}_2 + g(\tau)\,\dot{\mathbf{r}}_2, \quad f(\tau) = 1 - \frac{\mu}{2r_2^3}\tau^2 + \dots, \quad g(\tau) = \tau - \frac{\mu}{6r_2^3}\tau^3 + \dots$$
Symbol Key: $\tau = k(t - t_2)$ is Gaussian time difference, $\mu = 1$ in canonical solar units, and $r_2 = \|\mathbf{r}_2\|$ is heliocentric distance.

4. The Astrophysical Lesson: Precision vs. Accuracy

The Monte Carlo random measurement error ($\pm 0.005\text{ AU}$) was far smaller than the difference with NASA JPL. This proved our errors were not random noise, but systematic: the Gauss model assumes a pure two-body problem, ignoring planetary perturbations from Earth and Jupiter. Furthermore, because asteroid 2015 GF27 has a shallow inclination ($\sim 10^\circ$), the out-of-plane signal is tiny, dramatically amplifying orbital element sensitivities.

Quantum Field Theory in de Sitter Space: Foundations, Challenges, and Implications for Physics

🔮

The 10-Second Takeaway: In an exponentially expanding universe like our cosmos during inflation, empty space has no stable "nothingness"—an observer sees a thermal glow of radiating particles born from spacetime geometry itself. This monograph synthesizes how modern mathematical frameworks reconcile quantum unitarity with expanding cosmological horizons.

1. The Question: When Quantum Vacua Break Down

De Sitter space describes both the primordial inflation of the infant universe and today's dark-energy expansion. Yet ordinary quantum field theory breaks down here: there is no global timelike Killing vector, meaning there is no unique, canonical "empty vacuum." Instead, a continuous family of vacua (the $\alpha$-vacua) exists, static observers detect an unavoidable thermal Gibbons-Hawking radiation at temperature $T_{\rm dS} = H/2\pi$, and massless scalar fields develop infrared divergences that threaten perturbation theory.

2. Two Unifying Mathematical Frameworks

This work systematically unifies two advanced approaches:

  • Direct-Sum QFT: Replaces the single Hilbert space with $\mathcal{H}_+ \oplus \mathcal{H}_-$, separating time orientations. While the total cosmological state is strictly pure and unitary, an observer confined behind an event horizon sees a reduced density matrix $\rho_+ = \mathrm{Tr}_- |\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|$, which is mathematically identical to a thermal bath. Thermality is thus proven to be a direct consequence of horizon entanglement.
  • Ambient Space Formalism: Embeds 4D de Sitter space as a 5D hyperboloid $-X_0^2 + X_1^2 + \dots + X_4^2 = H^{-2}$ in flat Minkowski spacetime, allowing the symmetry group $\mathrm{SO}(1,4)$ to act by linear matrix operations, eliminating coordinate singularities and tracing infrared divergences to indecomposable group representations.
Cosmological Horizon Temperature & Ambient 5D Hyperboloid Natural Units ($\hbar = c = k_B = 1$)
$$T_{\rm dS} = \frac{H}{2\pi}, \qquad -X_0^2 + \sum_{i=1}^4 X_i^2 = H^{-2}, \qquad \mathcal{P}_\phi = \frac{H^2}{4\pi^2}$$
Symbol Key: $H$ is the Hubble expansion parameter, $T_{\rm dS}$ is the Gibbons-Hawking horizon temperature, and $\mathcal{P}_\phi$ is the scale-invariant scalar perturbation power spectrum that seeds galaxies in the cosmic microwave background.
3. Scientific Significance & Open Frontiers

The review connects these algebraic foundations directly to observable signatures in the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB). It concludes with a critical survey of unsolved open problems: constructing local, gauge-invariant observables in quantum gravity, the non-perturbative stability of de Sitter vacua in string theory, and the holographic dS/CFT correspondence.

From Homology to de Rham Cohomology: An Expository Journey through the Topology of Smooth Manifolds

📚

The 10-Second Takeaway: If you were an ant walking on a sphere versus a donut, every tiny patch would feel completely flat. How can calculus—which only measures tiny slopes—detect the global presence of holes? This 137-page book builds the path from Euler and Gauss to de Rham's theorem, proving calculus and topology are one and the same.

1. The Question: Detecting Shape Through Calculus

Local geometry cannot differentiate an open disc from a sphere: both are smooth and locally Euclidean. Yet globally, they possess fundamentally different topologies (holes, twists, boundaries). The foundational quest of modern differential topology is determining how calculus on smooth manifolds can compute invariant, global topological shapes.

2. The Method: The 137-Page Arc

Written as a self-contained graduate-level monograph, the text constructs the complete mathematical machinery across three stages:

  • Homology: Simplicial complexes, chain complexes, boundary operator $\partial$ with $\partial^2 = 0$, cycles modulo boundaries $H_k(M) = \ker \partial_k / \operatorname{im} \partial_{k+1}$, and singular homology.
  • Cohomology & Duality: Dual cochain complexes, coboundary operator $d$, cup products $\smile$, the Mayer–Vietoris exact sequence, and Poincaré duality.
  • Differential Forms: The exterior algebra $\bigwedge^k T^*M$, exterior derivative $d$ with $d^2 = 0$, pullback maps, and the generalized Stokes' Theorem $\int_M d\omega = \int_{\partial M} \omega$.
De Rham Isomorphism & Generalized Stokes' Theorem Canonical Duality
$$H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(M) \;\cong\; H^k(M; \mathbb{R}) \qquad \Longleftrightarrow \qquad \int_M d\omega = \int_{\partial M} \omega$$
The Deep Insight: The analytic vector space of closed differential forms modulo exact forms ($H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)$) is naturally isomorphic to the topological singular cohomology groups with real coefficients ($H^k(M;\mathbb{R})$).
3. Calculations & Significance

The treatise culminates in a complete proof of de Rham's Theorem, supported by explicit computations on $n$-spheres $S^n$, $n$-tori $T^n$, punctured Euclidean spaces $\mathbb{R}^n \setminus \{0\}$, real projective spaces $\mathbb{RP}^n$, and compact Riemann surfaces of genus $g$. It represents a comprehensive synthesis of modern geometric analysis.

Rediscovering a Formula for π

🖊

The 10-Second Takeaway: An arithmetic slip while computing $\pi$ by hand led to an unexpected series that converged to $1 + \pi/2$. Rather than throwing it away, I derived a fully elementary proof using only single-variable calculus, generalized it to a continuous family of rapid formulas, and found a series that gives 7 digits of $\pi$ in only 8 terms.

1. The Discovery & The Generalization

While manipulating series expansions for $\pi$ by hand, an algebraic error produced the unexpected sum: $$\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{2^n}{\binom{2n+1}{n+1}} = 1+\frac{\pi}{2}$$ Instead of discarding it, I generalized this discovery into an entire one-parameter family of infinite series $S(a) = \sum_{n \ge 0} \frac{a^n}{\binom{2n+1}{n+1}}$ valid for all real $|a| < 4$.

2. The Method: An Elementary Beta-Integral Route

While the closed form can be framed within heavy hypergeometric functions (${}_2F_1(2, 1; \tfrac{3}{2}; \tfrac{a}{4})$), the paper's contribution is a strictly elementary derivation using only integration by parts, the ratio test, and Euler's Beta integral identity: $$\frac{1}{\binom{2n+1}{n+1}} = (n+1)\int_0^1 t^n(1-t)^n\,dt$$ Summing under the integral sign produces a clean geometric series, yielding $\int_0^1 \frac{dt}{(1 - at(1-t))^2}$, which evaluates directly by completing the square.

General Closed-Form Family for All Real $|a| < 4$ Elementary Closed Form
$$S(a) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{a^n}{\binom{2n+1}{n+1}} = \frac{2}{4-a} + \frac{8}{(4-a)^{3/2}\sqrt{a}}\,\arctan\sqrt{\frac{a}{4-a}}$$
Special Exact Evaluations: $S(1) = \frac{2}{3} + \frac{4\pi}{9\sqrt{3}}$, $S(2) = 1 + \frac{\pi}{2}$, and $S(3) = 2 + \frac{8\pi}{3\sqrt{3}}$.
3. Geometric Convergence & Significance

The terms decay exponentially as $(a/4)^n$. While the classical Leibniz formula for $\pi$ crawls agonizingly (requiring $10^7$ terms for 7 decimal places), choosing the acceleration parameter $a = 2 - \sqrt{2}$ produces 7 correct digits of $\pi$ in just 8 terms. The paper demonstrates how curiosity and mathematical play turn unexpected errors into rigorous closed-form discoveries.

The Single-Generator Theory of Cycle-Decomposed Digit-Subtraction Dynamics

🔢

The 10-Second Takeaway: Kaprekar's constant (6174) is famous: arrange digits, subtract, and you always get 6174. But why? We generalize this to any number base and digit permutation, proving that seemingly unpredictable digit dynamics decouple into clean, independent cycles powered by a single modular multiplication generator.

1. The Question: Generalizing Kaprekar Beyond Base 10

In 1949, D.R. Kaprekar discovered that sorting the digits of any four-digit number in descending and ascending order and subtracting always reaches 6174 within 7 steps. But what happens in arbitrary bases $B \ge 2$, under arbitrary permutation symmetries $\pi \in S_k$, where digit positions rotate and subtract on disjoint cycles?

2. The Method: Decoupling by Disjoint Permutations

We prove that the global dynamical map decomposes into completely independent sub-systems—one for each disjoint cycle of $\pi$. On each cycle of length $t$, the subtraction map, after modulo-sign equivalence $x \sim -x$, reduces to multiplication by a single generator: $$c = (B - 1)B^{t-1} \pmod{B^t - 1}$$ Factoring the modulus $B^t - 1 = N_1 N_2$, where every prime dividing $N_1$ divides $B-1$ and $\gcd(N_2, B-1) = 1$, allows all dynamical behavior to be deduced analytically from $N_1$ and $N_2$.

The Single-Generator Algebraic Theorem Ring $\mathbb{Z}/(B^t - 1)\mathbb{Z}$
$$c = (B-1)B^{t-1} \pmod{B^t - 1}, \qquad \text{Total Attractor Rank} = \prod_{i} \frac{N_2(B, t_i) + 1}{2}$$
The Connection: For two-cycles ($t = 2$), the formula simplifies to $(\mathrm{odd}(B+1) + 1)/2$, which is identically the integer sequence OEIS A003602!
3. Structural Theorems & Significance
  • Attractor Cardinality: Each independent piece has exactly $(N_2 + 1)/2$ periodic attractor states.
  • Finite Transient Bound: Every initial state reaches a periodic orbit in either $a_0$ or $a_0 + 1$ iterations, where $a_0$ is the least integer with $N_1 \mid c^{a_0}$.
  • Trivial Collapse Criterion: Total collapse to zero occurs if and only if $t = 1$, or $t = 2$ when $B+1$ is a power of two.
  • Novel Complex Orbits: Discovered multi-cycle attractors—for example, Base 14 with cycle length 3 exhibits a cycle of period 105; Base 6 with length 4 hosts simultaneous cycles of length 1, 3, and 9.
