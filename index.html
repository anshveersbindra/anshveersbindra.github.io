Orbit Determination of Near-Earth Asteroid 2015 GF27 via the Method of Gauss

Astronomy With Chloe Leung and Gavin Flajole · Summer Science Program

In one line. Four of six orbital elements land within 0.6% of NASA's values from just three images; the larger gaps are systematic, not noise.

Question. How much of an asteroid's orbit can be recovered from just three observations? We used images of Near-Earth Asteroid 2015 GF27 taken with Las Cumbres Observatory telescopes in Chile, Hawaii, and Texas, spanning about 26 days.

Approach. After reducing the raw FITS CCD images and measuring the asteroid against 20 reference stars per image, we implemented Gauss's 1801 method in Python. The middle position is written as a combination of the outer two, \(\mathbf r_2=a_1\mathbf r_1+a_3\mathbf r_3\), and refined iteratively with fourth-order series until the solution converged to \(10^{-10}\text{ AU}\). Measurement noise was estimated from the reference stars and propagated via 1,000 Monte Carlo runs.

\[\mathbf r(\tau)=f(\tau)\,\mathbf r_2+g(\tau)\,\dot{\mathbf r}_2, \quad f(\tau)=1-\frac{\mu}{2r_2^3}\tau^2+\dots, \quad g(\tau)=\tau-\frac{\mu}{6r_2^3}\tau^3+\dots\]
$\tau = k(t - t_2)$ is Gaussian time difference, $\mu = 1$ in canonical solar units, and $r_2 = \|\mathbf{r}_2\|$ is heliocentric distance.

Results. Compared with NASA's JPL Horizons ephemerides:

ElementOursJPL HorizonsDifference
Semi-major axis ($a$)2.2932 AU2.3610 AU0.61%
Eccentricity ($e$)0.44360.45650.51%
Inclination ($i$)9.978°10.136°0.11%
Ascending node ($\Omega$)231.708°231.401°0.20%
Arg. of periapsis ($\omega$)46.348°47.143°2.35%
Mean anomaly ($M$)1.502°1.287°17.83%

Significance. The Monte Carlo spread ($\pm0.005$ AU in semi-major axis) is much smaller than these gaps, proving the errors are systematic and not noise. Two physical causes fit: the Gauss model ignores planetary perturbations from Earth and Jupiter, and with an orbit tilted only about $10^\circ$, the node and periapsis depend on a small out-of-plane signal that amplifies errors. The main lesson is that observational precision is not the same as physical accuracy.

A Weak-Form 2D Continuity Inversion for Pulsar Birth Demographics

Astrophysics With Tanish Kulkarni, 2026 · Paper I of the pulsar series

In one line. Only about 0.57% of pulsars are born slow (95% limit 25% to 30%), which rules out the classic claim that 40% or more are.

Question. Are neutron stars born spinning fast, or are many born slow? Neutron stars should start with spin periods of tens of milliseconds, but a classic 1981 argument (Vivekanand and Narayan) concluded that 40% to 75% of pulsars enter the population at slow periods of 0.4 to 0.85 s. Settling this from data is hard for two reasons. A pulsar's spin-down rate depends on its magnetic field as \(\dot P \propto B^2/P\), so at the same period it can vary by six orders of magnitude, and telescope selection hides most of the population in a way that is itself unknown.

Approach. Pulsars drift through the plane of period \(P\) and field \(B\) in a deterministic way, so the birth rate \(S\) must satisfy a continuity equation:

\[\nabla\!\cdot\!\big(\mathbf v\,f\big)+\Lambda f=S \quad \implies \quad \iint \Big[ \mathbf{v}f \cdot \nabla\phi - \Lambda f \phi + S\phi \Big] \,d(\log P)\,d(\log B) = 0\]
$\mathbf{v} = (\dot{P}, \dot{B})$ is the drift velocity, $f$ is population density, $\Lambda$ is the death cutoff rate, $S$ is birth rate, and $\phi$ is an infinitely smooth test function.

We solve for \(S\) from the observed catalogue. To avoid differentiating noisy histograms, the equation is written in weak form: integrating by parts against smooth test functions moves every derivative onto the test function. The unknown telescope visibility is calibrated only outside the slow-birth window of 0.40 to 0.85 s, so the result cannot be circular.

Validation. On 100 synthetic catalogues of 2,356 pulsars, the method recovered fast, Gaussian, and two-peaked birth distributions to within 0.06 dex in log period, with a resolution of 0.29 dex inside the slow window. Statistical exclusion limits were calibrated with 300 Monte Carlo runs per test.

Results. Applied to 2,356 isolated pulsars from the ATNF catalogue, the inversion finds that only 0.57% of pulsars are born slow (at most 9.03% across all field-decay rates), with formal 95% upper limits of 25% to 30%. Four independent checks that do not depend on the fitted visibility agree:

  • Supernova remnant census: at most 22%
  • Scale-height kinematic clock (drift from Galactic plane): at most 17%, in 2.6σ tension with 50% slow births
  • Volume-complete cylinders around the Sun: at most 4% to 9%
  • High-field strip conservation bound: at most 23%

The classical 40%-or-more hypothesis is excluded under every protocol. The paper is also explicit about its limit: slow-birth fractions below about 25% to 30% cannot be resolved with current data.

The Intrinsic Equilibrium Demographic Distribution of Galactic Pulsars

Astrophysics With Tanish Kulkarni, 2026 · Paper II of the pulsar series

In one line. Every pulsar is born fast, yet today's Milky Way population peaks near 1.6 s, because pulsars spend about 100,000 times longer at slow spins.

Question. Paper I shows that pulsars are born fast, with the birth rate peaking at about 48 ms. So what does the real population of the Milky Way look like today? The catalogue is a heavily filtered sample: only 2,356 timed pulsars, roughly 3% of the total.

Approach. We evolve the reconstructed birth distribution forward in time under dipole braking, slow decay of the crustal magnetic field, and a "death valley" where radio emission switches off. In steady state, the density \(f(\log P,\log B)\) obeys

\[\partial_x\!\big(v_x f\big)+\partial_y\!\big(v_y f\big)+\Lambda f=S,\qquad v_x\propto B^2P^{-(n-1)}, \qquad \tau_{\rm res}\propto \frac{P^2}{B^2}\]
$x = \log P$, $y = \log B$, $v_x$ is dipole braking, and $\tau_{\rm res}$ is residence time per decade of spin period.

Because information only flows right and down, it can be solved exactly in a single sweep. A separate Fortran simulation of 100,000 stars over 100 Myr provides an independent check, agreeing to \(W_1 = 0.028\) dex.

Why the result flips. The time a pulsar spends per decade of period grows as \(\tau_{\rm res}\propto P^2/B^2\), from about 270 years at birth to about 23 million years at maturity, a factor of \(10^5\). Pulsars cross the fast regime in under 0.1 Myr and then spend about 71% of their active lives between 0.5 and 3 s. The true population therefore peaks at 1.61 s with a median of 1.38 s, even though every pulsar was born fast. No slow injection is needed.

Results.

  • After applying telescope selection (beaming, luminosity, scattering), the model reproduces the observed catalogue: median 0.62 s, \(W_1 = 0.037\) dex.
  • The Galaxy hosts about \((0.82\pm0.27)\times10^5\) active radio pulsars and about \(1.1\times10^8\) dead radio pulsars.
  • High-latitude pulsars, which the model was not fitted to, are predicted by ballistic drift out of the disk: \(W_1=0.031\) dex, KS \(p=0.199\).
  • Adding slow injection makes the match worse, \(W_1\) rising from 0.037 to 0.112 dex at +50%.
  • Forecasts: discovery medians shift to 0.83 s for FAST and 1.03 s for SKA1-Mid.

The Single-Generator Theory of Cycle-Decomposed Digit-Subtraction Dynamics

Number theory August 2026 In review at Integers journal

In one line. A complete closed-form theory of cycles, transients and rank for generalized Kaprekar maps in any base.

Setting. Kaprekar's routine sorts the digits of a number, subtracts, and repeats; every four-digit number then lands on 6174. I study a generalization in any base \(B\): choose a permutation \(\pi\) of the digit positions, and on each cycle of \(\pi\) subtract the digit string from its rotation, then scatter the result back into place. For \(B=10\) and \(\pi=(1\,3)(2\,4)\), the number 5678 becomes 1188.

Structure. The map splits exactly into independent pieces, one per cycle, and each piece of length \(t\) is, after identifying \(x\sim -x\), multiplication by

\[c=(B-1)B^{t-1}\quad\text{on}\quad \mathbb Z/(B^t-1)\mathbb Z , \qquad \text{Eventual Rank} = \prod_i \frac{N_2(B,t_i)+1}{2}\]
Factor $B^t-1=N_1N_2$, where every prime of $N_1$ divides $B-1$ and $\gcd(N_2,B-1)=1$. Everything reads off from $N_1$ and $N_2$.

Results.

  • Each piece has exactly \((N_2+1)/2\) periodic states. Their cycle lengths are orders of \(c\) modulo divisors of \(N_2\), and the least common multiple of all lengths is the order of \(c\) in \((\mathbb Z/N_2)^\times/\{\pm1\}\).
  • Every state reaches a periodic one in \(a_0\) or \(a_0+1\) steps, where \(a_0\) is the least integer with \(N_1\mid c^{a_0}\).
  • Everything collapses to zero only for \(t=1\), or for \(t=2\) when \(B+1\) is a power of two.
  • The eventual rank of the whole map is the product \(\prod_i (N_2(B,t_i)+1)/2\) over the cycles. For two-cycles it equals \((\mathrm{odd}(B+1)+1)/2\), which is identically the integer sequence OEIS A003602.
  • Examples: for \(B=14,\ t=3\) the cycle structure has period 105; for \(B=6,\ t=4\), cycles of length 1, 3, and 9 coexist.

Rediscovering a Formula for π

Mathematics Elementary proof In review at Axiom journal

In one line. Proves $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{2^n}{\binom{2n+1}{n+1}} = 1 + \frac{\pi}{2}$, and a closed form for every $|a| < 4$, using only elementary calculus.

Origin. While computing \(\pi\) by hand from a series, I made a slip that produced a different series. Numerically it seemed to equal \(1+\pi/2\):

\[S(a)=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{a^n}{\binom{2n+1}{n+1}}=\frac{2}{4-a}+\frac{8}{(4-a)^{3/2}\sqrt a}\,\arctan\sqrt{\frac{a}{4-a}}\]
Evaluates to $S(1)=\frac{2}{3}+\frac{4\pi}{9\sqrt3}$, $S(2)=1+\frac{\pi}{2}$, and $S(3)=2+\frac{8\pi}{3\sqrt3}$.

Proof idea. A Beta-integral identity gives \(1/\binom{2n+1}{n+1}=(n+1)\int_0^1 t^n(1-t)^n\,dt\). The sum then becomes a geometric-type series inside the integral, leaving \(\int_0^1\frac{dt}{(1-a\,t(1-t))^2}\), which is evaluated by completing the square. Only factorials, integration by parts, and the ratio test are used. The closed form is known in the hypergeometric literature as \({}_2F_1(2,1;\tfrac32;\tfrac a4)\), so the contribution is the elementary route.

Speed. The terms shrink like \((a/4)^n\), geometrically, while the Leibniz series for \(\pi\) needs about \(10^k\) terms for \(k\) digits. Choosing \(a=2-\sqrt2\) gives seven correct digits of \(\pi\) after eight terms.

Quantum Field Theory in de Sitter Space: Foundations, Challenges, and Implications for Physics

Theoretical physics Review paper, 2026

In one line. The heat an observer sees in an expanding universe, at temperature $H/2\pi$, comes from entanglement across the horizon, so quantum theory stays unitary.

Question. De Sitter space, the geometry of a universe with constant positive curvature, describes both inflation and today's accelerated expansion. It is also where ordinary quantum field theory breaks down: there is no global time, so there is no canonical vacuum. A one-parameter family of equally valid vacua exists, and a static observer sees thermal radiation at temperature

\[T_{\rm dS}=\frac{H}{2\pi}, \qquad -X_0^2+\sum_{i=1}^4 X_i^2 = H^{-2}, \qquad \mathcal P_\phi = \frac{H^2}{4\pi^2}\]
$H$ is the Hubble expansion rate, $T_{\rm dS}$ is the Gibbons-Hawking temperature, and $\mathcal P_\phi$ is the primordial inflationary power spectrum.

Two frameworks.

  • Direct-sum QFT replaces the Hilbert space with \(\mathcal H_{+}\oplus\mathcal H_{-}\), one sector for each time orientation. The global state is pure, but an observer confined to one sector sees the reduced density matrix \(\rho_+=\mathrm{Tr}_-|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|\), which is exactly thermal. Thermality becomes a consequence of what the observer cannot access, and unitarity is preserved.
  • Ambient space formalism embeds de Sitter space as the hyperboloid \(-X_0^2+X_1^2+\dots+X_4^2=H^{-2}\) in five-dimensional flat space, so the symmetry group \(SO(1,4)\) acts by simple matrix multiplication. Fields become representations of this group, correlation functions depend only on one invariant, and the infrared divergence of the massless scalar traces back to an indecomposable representation.

Takeaways. The first framework explains why observers see heat, and the second explains which fields are consistent with the symmetry. Together they account for the scale-invariant inflationary power spectrum \(\mathcal P_\phi=H^2/4\pi^2\) seen in the cosmic microwave background. The paper closes with the open problems: interacting fields, gauge-invariant observables for gravitons, and whether de Sitter space can be stable in quantum gravity.

From Homology to de Rham Cohomology: An Expository Journey through the Topology of Smooth Manifolds

Topology 137-page exposition, 2026

In one line. A 137-page exposition culminating in de Rham's theorem: calculus-based and topological invariants of a manifold coincide.

Question. Local geometry cannot tell a sphere from a torus, yet the two are fundamentally different. How can calculus detect global shape? The exposition follows the thread from Euler, Poincaré, and Gauss–Bonnet to a modern answer.

Path. It builds homology (simplicial complexes, chains, cycles, boundaries, and singular homology), then cohomology (cochains, functoriality, relative cohomology, the Mayer–Vietoris sequence, and the cup product), then differential forms and the generalized Stokes theorem \(\int_M d\omega=\int_{\partial M}\omega\).

\[H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)\;\cong\;H^k(M;\mathbb R) \qquad \Longleftrightarrow \qquad \int_M d\omega = \int_{\partial M}\omega\]
Closed forms modulo exact forms are naturally isomorphic to singular cohomology groups over $\mathbb{R}$.

Destination. De Rham's theorem proves the analytic invariants equal the topological ones. The paper proves it, and computes explicit examples on spheres, tori, punctured Euclidean space, projective spaces, and surfaces of higher genus. The goal throughout is to present de Rham cohomology as the natural endpoint of a search for invariants linking local calculus to global topology.

String Quartet in E minor, “Future”

Composition 2025 · about 3 minutes · two violins, viola, cello · Andante into Adagio

In one line. The intervals between notes come from a number sequence reduced modulo 7 or 11; everything else is left to musical choice, and at measure 27 I deliberately break the rule.

How mathematical serialism works

  1. Start with a generating sequence, such as the Fibonacci numbers 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, …
  2. Read each term as an interval, the distance between two notes, not as a pitch.
  3. Reduce modulo a chosen number \(m\). Mod 7 gives seven interval classes and tonal, scale-based material; mod 11 gives eleven and chromatic, atonal material.
  4. Apply the intervals in turn. Each result sets the distance from one note to the next. Octave equivalence applies, so one interval class can sound in any octave.
  5. Direction is free. Mod \(m\), descending by \(a\) is the same as ascending by \(m-a\): in mod 7, down 3 equals up 4. A class \(a\) can also be taken up by \(a\) or down by \(a\).
  6. Build a transposition matrix. Its rows give related material: transpositions, retrogrades (backwards), inversions (mirrored), and retrograde inversions.

The system fixes the interval relationships only. Starting pitch, octave, rhythm, harmony, instrumentation, contour, and how material is shared between the four instruments are all composer’s choices.

The deliberate exception. In measure 27 the piece breaks the rule and adjusts the sequence so that the first movement can resolve in E minor. The score shows where.
