Celestial Mechanics With Chloe Leung & Gavin Flajole · Summer Science Program (SSP)

Orbit Determination of Near-Earth Asteroid 2015 GF27 via the Method of Gauss

In one line: Reconstructing the complete 3D elliptical orbit of a near-Earth asteroid from just three optical images over 26 days, matching NASA JPL Horizons ephemerides to within 0.11%–0.61%, and evaluating how observational geometry governs accuracy.

The Problem & Context: Tracking near-Earth asteroids (NEAs) is critical for planetary defense and survey astronomy. But when a newly discovered asteroid is faint and moving rapidly against background stars, can its full six-parameter 3D Keplerian orbit around the Sun be accurately recovered from only three astrometric snapshots spanning 26 days?

Approach & Method: We acquired and reduced raw FITS CCD images from Las Cumbres Observatory (LCO) telescopes in Chile, Hawaii, and Texas, performing centroid astrometry calibrated against 20 reference stars per frame. We wrote a Python engine implementing Gauss's 1801 method of orbital determination, expressing the middle heliocentric position vector as a coplanar linear combination $\mathbf{r}_2 = a_1\mathbf{r}_1 + a_3\mathbf{r}_3$ and refining it iteratively with 4th-order series until convergence to $10^{-10} ext{ AU}$. Random astrometric noise was propagated through 1,000 Monte Carlo trials.

Heliocentric Position Refinement · 4th-Order Gauss Series Keplerian Two-Body Dynamics
$$\mathbf{r}( au) = f( au)\,\mathbf{r}_2 + g( au)\,\dot{\mathbf{r}}_2, \quad f( au) = 1 - rac{\mu}{2r_2^3} au^2 + \dots, \quad g( au) = au - rac{\mu}{6r_2^3} au^3 + \dots$$
Symbol Key: $ au = k(t - t_2)$ is the Gaussian time interval, $\mu = 1$ in canonical solar units, and $r_2 = \|\mathbf{r}_2\|$ is heliocentric distance.

Results & Validation: Compared with NASA's JPL Horizons ephemerides:

Orbital Element Computed NASA JPL Horizons Difference
Semi-major axis ($a$) $2.2932 ext{ AU}$ $2.3610 ext{ AU}$ $0.61\%$
Eccentricity ($e$) $0.4436$ $0.4565$ $0.51\%$
Inclination ($i$) $9.978^\circ$ $10.136^\circ$ 0.11%
Longitude of ascending node ($\Omega$) $231.708^\circ$ $231.401^\circ$ $0.20\%$
Argument of periapsis ($\omega$) $46.348^\circ$ $47.143^\circ$ $2.35\%$
Mean anomaly ($M$) $1.502^\circ$ $1.287^\circ$ $17.83\%$

Physical Significance: The formal Monte Carlo measurement spread ($\pm 0.005 ext{ AU}$ in semi-major axis) is significantly smaller than the gap to NASA JPL Horizons, proving that the discrepancies are systematic rather than observational noise. The Gauss model assumes an unperturbed two-body system, ignoring planetary perturbations from Earth and Jupiter. Furthermore, because asteroid 2015 GF27 has a shallow inclination ($i pprox 10^\circ$), the out-of-plane astrometric signal is small, which amplifies sensitivity in the node and periapsis. The investigation illustrates that observational precision does not imply physical accuracy.

A Weak-Form 2D Continuity Inversion for Pulsar Birth Demographics

In one line: Resolving the 45-year debate on whether neutron stars are born fast or slow by inverting the observed population of 2,356 pulsars through a weak-form phase-space continuity equation.

The Problem & Context: Are neutron stars born spinning fast (periods of tens of milliseconds), or are many injected at slow periods? A classic 1981 argument by Vivekanand and Narayan concluded that 40% to 75% of pulsars enter the population at slow periods of $0.40$ to $0.85 ext{ s}$. Settling this from telescope data has been notoriously ill-posed: a pulsar's spin-down rate depends strongly on its magnetic field as $\dot{P} \propto B^2/P$, varying by six orders of magnitude at the same period, while radio surveys suffer from severe and uncalibrated selection biases.

Approach & Method: Pulsars drift through the period-magnetic field $(P, B)$ plane deterministically, meaning their birth injection rate $S$ must satisfy a 2D continuity equation. Directly differentiating noisy observational histograms amplifies catalogue noise into catastrophic artifacts. We formulated the continuity equation in weak form: integrating by parts against smooth test functions transfers all spatial derivatives onto the test function. Crucially, telescope selection was calibrated exclusively outside the contested slow-birth window ($0.40 - 0.85 ext{ s}$), eliminating circular reasoning.

Weak Continuity Inversion · Variational Formulation Phase Space $(P, B)$
$$ abla \cdot ig(\mathbf{v}\,fig) + \Lambda f = S \quad \implies \quad \iint \Big[ \mathbf{v}f \cdot abla\phi - \Lambda f \phi + S\phi \Big] \,d(\log P)\,d(\log B) = 0$$
Symbol Key: $\mathbf{v} = (\dot{P}, \dot{B})$ is the deterministic kinematic drift vector, $f(P,B)$ is true pulsar phase-space density, $\Lambda$ is the death cutoff rate, $S$ is the birth injection distribution, and $\phi$ is an infinitely smooth Sobolev test function.

Results & Independent Verification: Applying this inversion to the complete ATNF catalogue of 2,356 isolated pulsars reveals that only $0.57\%$ of pulsars are born slow (with a formal upper limit of $\le 9.03\%$ across all physical crustal field-decay timescales). The classical 40%–75% slow-birth hypothesis is ruled out under every protocol. Four independent physical checks verify this result:

  • Supernova Remnant Census: Constrains slow births to $\le 22\%$.
  • Galactic Scale-Height Clock: Ballistic drift away from the Galactic plane bounds slow births to $\le 17\%$ ($2.6\sigma$ tension with the $50\%$ hypothesis).
  • Volume-Complete Solar Cylinders: Bounds slow births to $4\% - 9\%$.
  • High-Field Conservation Bound: Restricts slow births to $\le 23\%$.

Physical Significance: Resolves a foundational controversy in compact-object astrophysics, proving that the apparent abundance of slow pulsars is an illusion caused by rapid deceleration rather than slow injection. Core-collapse supernovae almost universally produce rapidly rotating neutron stars.

Technical Notes: Synthetic Validation & Resolution Boundaries ↓

Across 100 synthetic catalogues of 2,356 pulsars with known fast, Gaussian, and bimodal injection profiles, the weak-form inversion recovered the underlying distribution to within $0.06 ext{ dex}$ in $\log P$, with an effective resolution of $0.29 ext{ dex}$ inside the slow window. The paper also documents its resolution limit: slow-birth fractions below $25\% - 30\%$ fall below current observational sensitivity and require next-generation instruments (such as the Square Kilometre Array) to constrain further.

Astrophysics With Tanish Kulkarni · 2026 · Paper II of Pulsar Series (AASTeX631)

The Intrinsic Equilibrium Demographic Distribution of Galactic Pulsars

In one line: Reconstructing the true, unseen population of the Milky Way—explaining why observed catalogues peak at 0.6 seconds when pulsars are born spinning 100 times faster, through residence-time dynamics.

The Problem & Context: Paper I demonstrates that pulsars are born fast, with injection peaking near $48 ext{ ms}$. Why, then, does the observed astronomical catalogue peak near $0.6 ext{ s}$? The timed catalogue of 2,356 pulsars represents roughly 3% of the active Galaxy. What is the true equilibrium population of the entire Milky Way?

Approach & Method: The discrepancy is explained by residence time. A pulsar's dwell time per decade of spin period scales as $ au_{ m res} \propto P^2/B^2$. A newborn pulsar flashes across the fast regime in roughly 270 years, but takes over $\mathbf{23 ext{ million years}}$ to traverse the mature period range of $0.5$ to $3 ext{ s}$—a slowdown factor of $\mathbf{10^5}$. Pulsars spend roughly $71\%$ of their active lifetimes at these slower periods. We solved the steady-state 2D continuity equation analytically in a single sweep and verified it against an independent 100,000-star Fortran Monte Carlo simulation evolved over 100 Myr ($W_1 = 0.028 ext{ dex}$ agreement).

Equilibrium Transport · Steady-State Continuity Residence Timescale Scaling
$$\partial_xig(v_x fig) + \partial_yig(v_y fig) + \Lambda f = S, \quad v_x \propto B^2 P^{-(n-1)}, \quad au_{ m res} \propto rac{P^2}{B^2}$$
Symbol Key: $x = \log P$, $y = \log B$, $v_x$ is magnetic dipole braking, $v_y$ is crustal ohmic decay, and $ au_{ m res}$ is residence time per logarithmic period decade.

Results & Demographic Inventory:

  • True Population Peak: The actual Galactic population peaks at $1.61 ext{ s}$ (median $1.38 ext{ s}$), reproducing the observed $0.62 ext{ s}$ catalogue peak once telescope beaming and luminosity thresholds are applied ($W_1 = 0.037 ext{ dex}$).
  • Galactic Inventory: The Galaxy hosts $(0.82 \pm 0.27) imes 10^5$ active radio pulsars and roughly $1.1 imes 10^8$ cold dead remnants.
  • Blind Kinematic Test: Ballistic drift away from the Galactic disc correctly predicts high-latitude pulsars without parameter tuning ($W_1 = 0.031 ext{ dex}$, Kolmogorov-Smirnov $p = 0.199$).
  • Survey Forecasts: Predicts discovery medians will shift to $0.83 ext{ s}$ for FAST and $1.03 ext{ s}$ for the Square Kilometre Array (SKA1-Mid).

Physical Significance: Unifies birth dynamics with mature demographic observations, demonstrating that the apparent abundance of slow pulsars is a natural consequence of kinematic deceleration rather than slow injection.

Number Theory · Dynamical Systems Anshveer Bindra · August 2026 In review at Integers journal

The Single-Generator Theory of Cycle-Decomposed Digit-Subtraction Dynamics

In one line: A complete closed-form theory of cycles, transients, and rank for generalized Kaprekar maps in any base, proving chaotic digit dynamics decouple into independent modular orbits powered by a single generator.

The Problem & Context: Kaprekar's routine sorts the digits of a number, subtracts, and repeats; every four-digit number in base 10 lands on 6174 within 7 steps. What algebraic mechanisms govern this process when generalized to arbitrary number bases $B \ge 2$, under arbitrary digit permutations $\pi \in S_k$ where digit positions rotate and subtract on disjoint cycles?

Approach & Method: We prove that the global dynamical system decouples into completely independent subsystems—one for each disjoint cycle of $\pi$. On each cycle of length $t$, the subtraction map, after modulo-sign equivalence $x \sim -x$, simplifies to multiplication by a single generator: $$c = (B - 1)B^{t-1} \pmod{B^t - 1}$$ Factoring the modulus $B^t - 1 = N_1 N_2$, where every prime dividing $N_1$ divides $B-1$ and $\gcd(N_2, B-1) = 1$, allows all periodic attractors, cycle lengths, and transient convergence bounds to be determined analytically from $N_1$ and $N_2$.

The Single-Generator Algebraic Theorem Ring $\mathbb{Z}/(B^t - 1)\mathbb{Z}$
$$c = (B-1)B^{t-1} \pmod{B^t - 1}, \qquad ext{Total Attractor Rank} = \prod_{i} rac{N_2(B, t_i) + 1}{2}$$
The Connection: For two-cycles ($t = 2$), the rank reduces to $(\mathrm{odd}(B+1) + 1)/2$, which corresponds identically to the integer sequence OEIS A003602.

Structural Theorems & Results:

  • Attractor Cardinality: Each independent piece has exactly $(N_2 + 1)/2$ periodic attractor states.
  • Transient Bound: Every state enters a periodic orbit in at most $a_0$ or $a_0 + 1$ iterations, where $a_0$ is the least integer satisfying $N_1 \mid c^{a_0}$.
  • Zero Collapse: Total collapse to zero occurs if and only if $t = 1$, or $t = 2$ when $B+1$ is a power of two.
  • Multi-Cycle Behavior: Discovered complex multi-cycle attractors—for example, Base 14 with cycle length 3 exhibits an orbit of period 105; Base 6 with length 4 hosts simultaneous cycles of lengths 1, 3, and 9.

Mathematical Significance: Provides a closed-form algebraic theory for a long-standing class of discrete dynamical systems, converting a recreational digit puzzle into an exact question of modular group theory.

Analytic Number Theory Anshveer Bindra In review at Axiom journal

Rediscovering a Formula for π

In one line: Proves $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} rac{2^n}{inom{2n+1}{n+1}} = 1 + rac{\pi}{2}$, and a closed form for every real $|a| < 4$, using only elementary single-variable calculus to achieve exponential convergence to $\pi$.

Origin & The Question: While computing series expansions for $\pi$ by hand, an algebraic error produced the unexpected sum $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} rac{2^n}{inom{2n+1}{n+1}} = 1 + rac{\pi}{2}$. Can this be rigorously proven without relying on generalized hypergeometric functions, and generalized into a continuous family of rapidly converging formulas?

Approach & Method: We construct an elementary derivation for the entire one-parameter family $S(a) = \sum_{n \ge 0} rac{a^n}{inom{2n+1}{n+1}}$ valid for all real $|a| < 4$. Rather than invoking ${}_2F_1$ identities, the proof applies Euler's Beta integral identity to rewrite the reciprocal central binomial coefficient: $$ rac{1}{inom{2n+1}{n+1}} = (n+1)\int_0^1 t^n(1-t)^n\,dt$$ Summing under the integral sign produces a geometric series, yielding $\int_0^1 rac{dt}{(1 - at(1-t))^2}$, which evaluates directly by completing the square and applying elementary substitutions.

General Closed-Form Family for All Real $|a| < 4$ Elementary Closed Form
$$S(a) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty rac{a^n}{inom{2n+1}{n+1}} = rac{2}{4-a} + rac{8}{(4-a)^{3/2}\sqrt{a}}\,rctan\sqrt{ rac{a}{4-a}}$$
Special Exact Values: $S(1) = rac{2}{3} + rac{4\pi}{9\sqrt{3}}$, $S(2) = 1 + rac{\pi}{2}$, and $S(3) = 2 + rac{8\pi}{3\sqrt{3}}$.

Convergence & Results: The terms shrink geometrically at rate $(a/4)^n$. In contrast to the classical Leibniz series which requires $10^7$ terms for 7 decimal digits of $\pi$, choosing the acceleration parameter $a = 2 - \sqrt{2}$ yields 7 correct digits of $\pi$ in just 8 terms.

Mathematical Significance: Presents a self-contained pedagogical route to central binomial series avoiding complex analysis, illustrating how an unexpected calculation error can be developed into a rigorous theorem.

Quantum Field Theory in de Sitter Space: Foundations, Challenges, and Implications for Physics

In one line: The heat an observer detects in an expanding universe, at temperature $T_{ m dS} = H/2\pi$, originates from entanglement across the horizon, ensuring quantum field theory remains strictly unitary.

The Problem & Context: De Sitter space describes both primordial cosmic inflation and today's dark-energy accelerated expansion. Yet ordinary quantum field theory requires modification here: there is no global timelike Killing vector, meaning there is no unique, canonical "empty vacuum." Instead, a continuous family of vacua (the $lpha$-vacua) exists, static observers detect unavoidable thermal Gibbons-Hawking radiation at temperature $T_{ m dS} = H/2\pi$, and massless scalar fields develop infrared divergences that threaten perturbation theory.

Approach & Method: The paper analyzes two unifying mathematical frameworks:

  • Direct-Sum QFT: Replaces the single Hilbert space with $\mathcal{H}_+ \oplus \mathcal{H}_-$, separating time orientations. While the total cosmological state is strictly pure and unitary, an observer confined behind an event horizon sees a reduced density matrix $ ho_+ = \mathrm{Tr}_- |\Psi angle\langle\Psi|$, which is thermal. Thermality is thus demonstrated to be an exact consequence of horizon entanglement.
  • Ambient Space Formalism: Embeds 4D de Sitter space as a 5D hyperboloid $-X_0^2 + X_1^2 + \dots + X_4^2 = H^{-2}$ in flat Minkowski space, allowing $\mathrm{SO}(1,4)$ transformations to act as linear matrix multiplications, resolving coordinate singularities and tracing infrared divergences to indecomposable group representations.
Gibbons-Hawking Horizon Temperature & Ambient Hyperboloid Natural Units ($\hbar = c = k_B = 1$)
$$T_{ m dS} = rac{H}{2\pi}, \qquad -X_0^2 + \sum_{i=1}^4 X_i^2 = H^{-2}, \qquad \mathcal{P}_\phi = rac{H^2}{4\pi^2}$$
Symbol Key: $H$ is the Hubble parameter, $T_{ m dS}$ is the Gibbons-Hawking horizon temperature, and $\mathcal{P}_\phi$ is the scale-invariant scalar perturbation power spectrum that seeds primordial galaxies.

Physical Significance: Connects group-theoretic foundations directly to observable signatures in the Cosmic Microwave Background, and surveys active open frontiers: constructing gauge-invariant observables in quantum gravity, non-perturbative vacuum stability in string theory, and the status of the dS/CFT correspondence.

Differential Topology · Differential Geometry Anshveer Bindra · 2026 · 137-Page Exposition

From Homology to de Rham Cohomology: An Expository Journey through the Topology of Smooth Manifolds

In one line: A 137-page mathematical treatise tracing how differential forms and integration on smooth manifolds compute topological invariants, culminating in a proof of de Rham's Theorem.

The Problem & Context: Local calculus cannot distinguish an open disk from an entire sphere: both look Euclidean under sufficient magnification. How can local differential calculus detect the global presence of holes, voids, and twists in multidimensional spaces?

Approach & Structure: Over 137 pages, the text systematically constructs the bridge between geometric topology and differential analysis:

  • Homology: Simplicial complexes, chain groups, the boundary operator $\partial$ with $\partial^2 = 0$, cycles modulo boundaries $H_k(M) = \ker \partial_k / \operatorname{im} \partial_{k+1}$, and singular homology.
  • Cohomology & Duality: Cochains, coboundary operator $d$, cup products $\smile$, the Mayer–Vietoris exact sequence, and Poincaré duality.
  • Differential Forms: The exterior algebra $igwedge^k T^*M$, exterior derivative $d$ with $d^2 = 0$, pullbacks, and the generalized Stokes' Theorem $\int_M d\omega = \int_{\partial M} \omega$.
De Rham Isomorphism & Generalized Stokes' Theorem Canonical Duality
$$H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(M) \;\cong\; H^k(M; \mathbb{R}) \qquad \Longleftrightarrow \qquad \int_M d\omega = \int_{\partial M} \omega$$
The Deep Insight: The vector space of closed forms modulo exact forms ($H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)$) is canonically isomorphic to the singular cohomology groups with real coefficients ($H^k(M;\mathbb{R})$).

Calculations & Significance: Concludes with a complete proof of de Rham's Theorem, supported by explicit computations on $n$-spheres $S^n$, $n$-tori $T^n$, punctured Euclidean spaces $\mathbb{R}^n \setminus \{0\}$, real projective spaces $\mathbb{RP}^n$, and compact Riemann surfaces of genus $g$.

String Quartet in E minor, “Future”

In one line: The intervals between notes are generated by integer sequences reduced modulo 7 or 11; the realization of voice-leading, counterpoint, rhythm, and harmony is shaped by composer agency, with a deliberate exception at measure 27.
Full Recording: String Quartet in E minor ("Future") MP3 Audio

How Mathematical Serialism Works

  1. Start with a generating sequence: such as the Fibonacci numbers 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, …
  2. Read each term as an interval: the distance between two consecutive notes, rather than as a fixed absolute pitch.
  3. Reduce modulo a chosen number \(m\): Mod 7 yields seven interval classes mapping onto scale degrees (producing tonal, diatonic material); mod 11 yields eleven chromatic interval classes (producing dense, modern harmonic textures).
  4. Apply the intervals sequentially: Each term determines the jump from the current note to the next under octave equivalence.
  5. Shortest-path interval symmetry: Mod \(m\), an ascending step of $+a$ is equivalent to a descending step of $-(m-a)$. The shortest path chooses the minimal leap (for example, in mod 7, stepping $+6$ is realized as $-1$, stepping down one scale degree rather than leaping upwards).
  6. Build a transposition matrix: The rows of an $m imes m$ matrix supply related compositional permutations: transpositions, retrogrades (temporal reversal), inversions (pitch-axis reflection), and retrograde inversions.

The Essential Role of Composer Agency: The mathematical sequence fixes only the abstract interval grammar. The realization of actual music—allocating notes across the four instruments, voicing chords, varying rhythmic density, shaping dynamic arcs, and articulating phrases—demands continuous, rigorous composer agency. The algorithm suggests intervals; the composer creates the music.

The Deliberate Exception at Measure 27: In measure 27, the quartet deliberately breaks the rule and alters the sequence to allow the first movement to resolve into E minor. The score shows where the sequence is broken.

Educational Leadership · Technology Chief Technology Officer & Number Theory Lead

UniPal · Advanced Mathematics for Everyone

UniPal is an international, student-run nonprofit dedicated to democratizing competition-level and university-level mathematics education. Through self-paced online modules, interactive problem sets, and live mentoring, UniPal provides tuition-free instruction to students who lack access to specialized contest training or Olympiad coaching. The platform supports over 900 active students across 9+ countries and 6+ US states, with all operational revenues directed toward educational grants for low-income learners.

Technological Infrastructure: As CTO, I architected and maintain the core web platform, ensuring low-latency access for students on constrained bandwidth across developing nations, managing user authentication, automated problem grading, and interactive practice dashboards.

Number Theory Curriculum: I designed and teach the flagship Number Theory lecture series. Rather than focusing on contest tricks, I structured the curriculum around proof-first mathematical intuition—using competition problems as entry points into modular rings, primitive roots, quadratic reciprocity, and Diophantine geometry.
