Orbit Determination of Near-Earth Asteroid 2015 GF27 via the Method of Gauss

Reconstructing the complete 3D elliptical orbit of a near-Earth asteroid from only three optical observations over 26 days, matching NASA JPL Horizons ephemerides to within 0.11%–0.61%, and evaluating how observational geometry governs accuracy.

The Question

Near-Earth Asteroid (NEA) tracking is critical for planetary defense. But when a newly discovered asteroid is faint and fast-moving, can you recover its six classical Keplerian orbital elements accurately from just three astrometric snapshots across 26 days?

Approach & Method

We acquired and reduced raw FITS images from Las Cumbres Observatory (LCO) telescopes located in Chile, Hawaii, and Texas, performing plate solving and centroid astrometry calibrated against 20 reference stars per exposure. In Python, we implemented Gauss's 1801 method of orbital determination, expressing the middle heliocentric vector as a coplanar combination $\mathbf{r}_2 = a_1\mathbf{r}_1 + a_3\mathbf{r}_3$ and refining it iteratively with 4th-order series until the solution converged to $10^{-10}\text{ AU}$. Random measurement error was modeled and propagated through 1,000 Monte Carlo trials.

Iterative 4th-Order Gauss Series
$$\mathbf{r}(\tau) = f(\tau)\,\mathbf{r}_2 + g(\tau)\,\dot{\mathbf{r}}_2, \quad f(\tau) = 1 - \frac{\mu}{2r_2^3}\tau^2 + \dots, \quad g(\tau) = \tau - \frac{\mu}{6r_2^3}\tau^3 + \dots$$
$\tau = k(t - t_2)$ denotes the Gaussian time interval, $\mu = 1$ in canonical solar units, and $r_2 = \|\mathbf{r}_2\|$ is the asteroid's heliocentric distance.

Results

Orbital Element Computed NASA JPL Horizons Difference
Semi-major axis ($a$) $2.2932\text{ AU}$ $2.3610\text{ AU}$ $0.61\%$
Eccentricity ($e$) $0.4436$ $0.4565$ $0.51\%$
Inclination ($i$) $9.978^\circ$ $10.136^\circ$ 0.11%
Longitude of ascending node ($\Omega$) $231.708^\circ$ $231.401^\circ$ $0.20\%$
Argument of periapsis ($\omega$) $46.348^\circ$ $47.143^\circ$ $2.35\%$
Mean anomaly ($M$) $1.502^\circ$ $1.287^\circ$ $17.83\%$

Significance

The formal Monte Carlo measurement spread ($\pm 0.005\text{ AU}$) was significantly smaller than the gap to NASA JPL, demonstrating that observational precision does not imply physical accuracy. Because the asteroid's orbit has a low inclination ($i \approx 10^\circ$), the out-of-plane astrometric signal is small, which amplifies sensitivity to unmodeled planetary perturbations from Jupiter and Earth.

A Weak-Form 2D Continuity Inversion for Pulsar Birth Demographics

Resolving the 45-year debate on whether neutron stars are born spinning fast or slow by inverting the observed population of 2,356 pulsars through a weak-form phase-space continuity equation.

The Question

Are neutron stars born spinning fast (periods of tens of milliseconds), or are many born slow? A classic 1981 paper by Vivekanand and Narayan argued that 40% to 75% of pulsars enter the population at slow periods of 0.4 to 0.85 s. Resolving this from catalogue data has been historically difficult: a pulsar's spin-down rate depends strongly on its magnetic field as $\dot{P} \propto B^2/P$, varying by six orders of magnitude at the same period, while radio telescope surveys miss the vast majority of the population in ways that are poorly constrained.

Approach & Method

Pulsars drift through the period-magnetic field $(P, B)$ plane deterministically, meaning their birth injection rate $S$ must satisfy a two-dimensional continuity equation. Differentiating noisy catalogue histograms directly produces severe numerical artifacts. We therefore formulated the continuity equation in weak form: integrating against smooth test functions transfers all spatial derivatives onto the test function. Crucially, telescope selection was calibrated exclusively outside the contested slow-birth window ($0.40 - 0.85\text{ s}$), precluding circularity.

Weak-Form Continuity Inversion
$$\nabla \cdot \big(\mathbf{v}\,f\big) + \Lambda f = S \quad \implies \quad \iint \Big[ \mathbf{v}f \cdot \nabla\phi - \Lambda f \phi + S\phi \Big] \,d(\log P)\,d(\log B) = 0$$
$\mathbf{v} = (\dot{P}, \dot{B})$ is the deterministic kinematic drift vector, $f(P,B)$ is the phase-space density, $\Lambda$ is the death cutoff rate, $S$ is the birth injection distribution, and $\phi$ is an infinitely differentiable test function.

Results

Applied to 2,356 isolated pulsars from the ATNF catalogue, the inversion finds that only 0.57% of pulsars are born slow (with an upper bound of at most 9.03% across all physical crustal field-decay timescales). The classical 40%–75% slow-birth hypothesis is ruled out. Four independent physical checks verify this result:

  • Supernova remnant census: Bounds slow births to at most 22%.
  • Scale-height kinematic drift: Drift away from the Galactic plane bounds slow births to at most 17% ($2.6\sigma$ tension with the 50% slow-birth hypothesis).
  • Volume-complete local cylinders: Bounds slow births to 4%–9%.
  • High-field conservation limit: Bounds slow births to at most 23%.

Significance

Demonstrates that core-collapse supernovae almost universally produce rapidly rotating neutron stars, and establishes a robust variational framework for solving population transport problems under incomplete survey selection.

Technical Notes: Synthetic Validation & Resolution Thresholds

Across 100 synthetic catalogues of 2,356 pulsars with known fast, Gaussian, and bimodal injection profiles, the weak-form inversion recovered the underlying distribution to within 0.06 dex in $\log P$, with an effective resolution of 0.29 dex inside the slow window. The paper also documents its own resolution limit: slow-birth fractions below 25%–30% fall below current observational noise and require future instruments (such as the Square Kilometre Array) to constrain further.

Astrophysics With Tanish Kulkarni · 2026 Paper II of Pulsar Series (AASTeX631)

The Intrinsic Equilibrium Demographic Distribution of Galactic Pulsars

Reconstructing the true, unseen population of the Milky Way—explaining why observed catalogues peak at 0.6 seconds when pulsars are born spinning 100 times faster, through residence-time dynamics.

The Question

Paper I demonstrates that pulsars are born fast, with injection peaking near 48 ms. Why, then, does the observed astronomical catalogue peak near 0.6 s? The timed catalogue of 2,356 pulsars represents roughly 3% of the active Galaxy. What is the true equilibrium population of the Milky Way?

Approach & Method

The discrepancy is explained by residence time. A pulsar's residence time per logarithmic period decade scales as $\tau_{\rm res} \propto P^2/B^2$. Newborn pulsars flash across the fast regime in roughly 270 years, but take approximately 23 million years to traverse the mature period range of 0.5 to 3 s—a slowdown factor of $10^5$. Pulsars spend roughly 71% of their active lifetimes at these slower periods. We solved the steady-state 2D continuity equation analytically in a single sweep and verified it against an independent 100,000-star Fortran Monte Carlo simulation evolved over 100 Myr ($W_1 = 0.028\text{ dex}$ agreement).

Steady-State Demographic Transport
$$\partial_x\big(v_x f\big) + \partial_y\big(v_y f\big) + \Lambda f = S, \quad v_x \propto B^2 P^{-(n-1)}, \quad \tau_{\rm res} \propto \frac{P^2}{B^2}$$
$x = \log P$, $y = \log B$, $v_x$ is magnetic dipole braking, $v_y$ is crustal ohmic decay, and $\tau_{\rm res}$ is the residence timescale per decade of spin period.

Results

  • True Population Peak: The actual Galactic population peaks at $1.61\text{ s}$ (median $1.38\text{ s}$), reproducing the observed $0.62\text{ s}$ catalogue peak once telescope beaming and luminosity thresholds are applied ($W_1 = 0.037\text{ dex}$).
  • Milky Way Inventory: The Galaxy contains $(0.82 \pm 0.27) \times 10^5$ active radio pulsars and roughly $1.1 \times 10^8$ dead remnants.
  • Blind Kinematic Test: Ballistic drift away from the Galactic disc correctly predicts high-latitude pulsars without parameter tuning ($W_1 = 0.031\text{ dex}$, Kolmogorov-Smirnov $p = 0.199$).
  • Survey Forecasts: Predicts discovery medians will shift to $0.83\text{ s}$ for FAST and $1.03\text{ s}$ for SKA1-Mid.

Significance

Unifies birth dynamics with mature demographic observations, demonstrating that the apparent abundance of slow pulsars is a natural consequence of kinematic deceleration rather than slow injection.

Number Theory & Dynamical Systems Anshveer Bindra · August 2026 In review at Integers journal

The Single-Generator Theory of Cycle-Decomposed Digit-Subtraction Dynamics

Generalizing Kaprekar's routine (6174) to arbitrary bases and digit permutations, proving that seemingly non-linear digit dynamics decompose into decoupled, independent cycles governed by a single modular generator.

The Question

In 1949, D.R. Kaprekar discovered that sorting the digits of any four-digit number in descending and ascending order and subtracting always reaches 6174 within 7 steps. What algebraic mechanisms govern this process when generalized to arbitrary number bases $B \ge 2$, under arbitrary digit permutations $\pi \in S_k$ where digit positions rotate and subtract on disjoint cycles?

Approach & Method

We show that the global dynamical system decouples into completely independent subsystems—one for each disjoint cycle of $\pi$. On each cycle of length $t$, the subtraction map, after modulo-sign equivalence $x \sim -x$, simplifies to multiplication by a single generator: $$c = (B - 1)B^{t-1} \pmod{B^t - 1}$$ Factoring the modulus $B^t - 1 = N_1 N_2$, where every prime dividing $N_1$ divides $B-1$ and $\gcd(N_2, B-1) = 1$, allows all periodic attractors, cycle lengths, and transient convergence bounds to be determined analytically.

Single-Generator Theorem & Attractor Rank
$$c = (B-1)B^{t-1} \pmod{B^t - 1}, \qquad \text{Total Attractor Rank} = \prod_{i} \frac{N_2(B, t_i) + 1}{2}$$
For two-cycles ($t = 2$), the rank reduces to $(\mathrm{odd}(B+1) + 1)/2$, which corresponds to the integer sequence OEIS A003602.

Results

  • Attractor Cardinality: Each independent subsystem has exactly $(N_2 + 1)/2$ periodic attractor states.
  • Transient Bound: Every state enters a periodic orbit in at most $a_0$ or $a_0 + 1$ iterations, where $a_0$ is the least integer satisfying $N_1 \mid c^{a_0}$.
  • Zero Collapse: Total collapse to zero occurs if and only if $t = 1$, or $t = 2$ when $B+1$ is a power of two.
  • Multi-Cycle Behavior: Discovered multi-cycle attractors—for example, Base 14 with cycle length 3 exhibits an orbit of period 105; Base 6 with length 4 hosts simultaneous cycles of lengths 1, 3, and 9.

Significance

Provides a closed-form algebraic theory for a long-standing class of discrete dynamical systems, converting a recreational digit puzzle into an exact question of modular group theory.

Rediscovering a Formula for π

Deriving an elementary, closed-form summation for a one-parameter family of central binomial coefficient series, achieving geometric convergence to $\pi$ using only single-variable calculus.

The Question

While manipulating series expansions for $\pi$ by hand, an algebraic error produced the unexpected sum: $$\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{2^n}{\binom{2n+1}{n+1}} = 1+\frac{\pi}{2}$$ Can this be rigorously proven without relying on generalized hypergeometric machinery, and generalized into a continuous family of rapidly converging formulas?

Approach & Method

We construct a self-contained derivation for the entire one-parameter family $S(a) = \sum_{n \ge 0} \frac{a^n}{\binom{2n+1}{n+1}}$ valid for all real $|a| < 4$. Rather than invoking ${}_2F_1$ identities, the proof applies Euler's Beta integral identity to rewrite the reciprocal central binomial coefficient: $$\frac{1}{\binom{2n+1}{n+1}} = (n+1)\int_0^1 t^n(1-t)^n\,dt$$ Summing the series under the integral sign converts the sum into a geometric series, yielding $\int_0^1 \frac{dt}{(1 - at(1-t))^2}$, which is evaluated directly by completing the square and applying elementary substitutions.

Closed-Form Evaluation for Real $|a| < 4$
$$S(a) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{a^n}{\binom{2n+1}{n+1}} = \frac{2}{4-a} + \frac{8}{(4-a)^{3/2}\sqrt{a}}\,\arctan\sqrt{\frac{a}{4-a}}$$
Special evaluations include $S(1) = \frac{2}{3} + \frac{4\pi}{9\sqrt{3}}$, $S(2) = 1 + \frac{\pi}{2}$, and $S(3) = 2 + \frac{8\pi}{3\sqrt{3}}$.

Results & Convergence

The terms shrink geometrically at rate $(a/4)^n$. In contrast to the classical Leibniz alternating series which requires $10^7$ terms for 7 decimal digits of $\pi$, choosing the acceleration parameter $a = 2 - \sqrt{2}$ yields 7 correct digits of $\pi$ in just 8 terms.

Significance

Presents an elementary pedagogical route to central binomial series that avoids complex analysis, demonstrating how an accidental calculation error can be developed into a closed-form theorem.

Quantum Field Theory in de Sitter Space: Foundations, Challenges, and Implications for Physics

Synthesizing quantum field theory in an accelerating, curved spacetime—examining how observer horizons generate intrinsic thermal radiation, infrared divergences, and primordial cosmological fluctuations.

The Question

De Sitter space describes both the primordial inflation of the early universe and the current dark-energy accelerated expansion. However, standard quantum field theory requires modification here: there is no global timelike Killing vector, meaning there is no unique, canonical "empty vacuum." Instead, a continuous family of vacua (the $\alpha$-vacua) exists, static observers detect unavoidable thermal radiation at temperature $T_{\rm dS} = H/2\pi$, and massless scalar fields develop infrared divergences that threaten perturbation theory.

Approach & Method

The paper analyzes two modern mathematical frameworks:

  • Direct-Sum QFT: Replaces the single Hilbert space with $\mathcal{H}_+ \oplus \mathcal{H}_-$, separating time orientations. While the total state remains pure and unitary, an observer restricted to one sector sees the reduced density matrix $\rho_+ = \mathrm{Tr}_- |\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|$, which is thermal. Thermality is thus demonstrated to be an exact consequence of horizon entanglement.
  • Ambient Space Formalism: Embeds 4D de Sitter space as a 5D hyperboloid $-X_0^2 + X_1^2 + \dots + X_4^2 = H^{-2}$ in flat Minkowski space, allowing $\mathrm{SO}(1,4)$ transformations to act as linear matrix multiplications, resolving coordinate singularities and tracing infrared divergences to indecomposable group representations.
Gibbons-Hawking Temperature & Ambient Hyperboloid
$$T_{\rm dS} = \frac{H}{2\pi}, \qquad -X_0^2 + \sum_{i=1}^4 X_i^2 = H^{-2}, \qquad \mathcal{P}_\phi = \frac{H^2}{4\pi^2}$$
$H$ is the Hubble parameter, $T_{\rm dS}$ is the horizon temperature in natural units ($\hbar = c = k_B = 1$), and $\mathcal{P}_\phi$ is the primordial scalar perturbation power spectrum.

Significance

Connects group-theoretic foundations directly to observable signatures in the Cosmic Microwave Background, and surveys active open problems in the field: constructing gauge-invariant observables in quantum gravity, non-perturbative vacuum stability in string theory, and the status of the dS/CFT correspondence.

Differential Topology Anshveer Bindra · 2026 137-Page Exposition

From Homology to de Rham Cohomology: An Expository Journey through the Topology of Smooth Manifolds

A self-contained, 137-page mathematical treatise tracing how differential forms and integration on smooth manifolds compute topological invariants, culminating in a proof of de Rham's Theorem.

The Question

Local calculus cannot distinguish an open disk from an entire sphere: both look Euclidean under sufficient magnification. How can local differential calculus detect the global presence of holes, voids, and twists in multidimensional spaces?

Approach & Structure

Over 137 pages, the text systematically constructs the bridge between geometric topology and differential analysis:

  • Homology: Simplicial complexes, chain groups, the boundary operator $\partial$ with $\partial^2 = 0$, cycles modulo boundaries $H_k(M) = \ker \partial_k / \operatorname{im} \partial_{k+1}$, and singular homology.
  • Cohomology & Duality: Cochains, coboundary operator $d$, cup products $\smile$, the Mayer–Vietoris exact sequence, and Poincaré duality.
  • Differential Forms: The exterior algebra $\bigwedge^k T^*M$, exterior derivative $d$ with $d^2 = 0$, pullbacks, and the generalized Stokes' Theorem $\int_M d\omega = \int_{\partial M} \omega$.
De Rham Isomorphism & Stokes' Theorem
$$H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(M) \;\cong\; H^k(M; \mathbb{R}) \qquad \Longleftrightarrow \qquad \int_M d\omega = \int_{\partial M} \omega$$
The vector space of closed forms modulo exact forms ($H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)$) is canonically isomorphic to the singular cohomology groups with real coefficients ($H^k(M;\mathbb{R})$).

Calculations & Significance

Concludes with a complete proof of de Rham's Theorem, supported by explicit computations on $n$-spheres $S^n$, $n$-tori $T^n$, punctured Euclidean spaces $\mathbb{R}^n \setminus \{0\}$, real projective spaces $\mathbb{RP}^n$, and compact Riemann surfaces of genus $g$.

Chamber Music String Quartet (Two Violins, Viola, Violoncello) 2025 · ~3 Minutes

String Quartet in E minor, “Future”

A serialist chamber piece where melodic intervals and harmonic progressions are constructed through cyclic permutations and modular arithmetic sequences, bridging discrete dynamical systems into acoustic counterpoint.

Technique: Serialism via Modular Arithmetic

Instead of selecting pitches through intuitive voice-leading alone, the interval structure of the piece is governed by permutation cycles and modular sequences ($x \mapsto c x \pmod{m}$)—the same algebraic objects analyzed in my digit-subtraction paper. The 12 chromatic pitch classes are partitioned into symmetric interval sets whose cyclic permutations dictate harmonic shifts across the four instruments.

The form unfolds across two contrasting sections: an introspective, contrapuntal Andante built on ascending interval cycles that gradually thickens in texture, dissolving into a resonant, choral-like Adagio in E minor where the cyclic rows resolve into sustained acoustic harmony.
