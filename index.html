Orbit Determination of Near-Earth Asteroid 2015 GF27 via the Method of Gauss

AstronomyWith Chloe Leung and Gavin Flajole · Summer Science Program
In one line. Four of six orbital elements land within 0.11%–0.61% of NASA's values from just three telescope images; the larger gaps are systematic, not noise.

Question. How much of a near-Earth asteroid's 3D orbit can be recovered from just three optical observations? We reduced images from Las Cumbres Observatory (LCO) telescopes in Chile, Hawaii, and Texas, spanning about 26 days.

Approach. After plate-solving raw FITS images and measuring the asteroid against 20 reference stars per frame, we implemented Gauss's 1801 method in Python. The middle position vector is written as a combination of the outer two, $\mathbf{r}_2 = a_1\mathbf{r}_1 + a_3\mathbf{r}_3$, and refined iteratively with fourth-order series until the solution converged to $10^{-10}\text{ AU}$:

$$\mathbf{r}(\tau) = f(\tau)\,\mathbf{r}_2 + g(\tau)\,\dot{\mathbf{r}}_2, \quad f(\tau) = 1 - \frac{\mu}{2r_2^3}\tau^2 + \dots, \quad g(\tau) = \tau - \frac{\mu}{6r_2^3}\tau^3 + \dots$$
Where $\tau = k(t - t_2)$ is the Gaussian time interval, $\mu = 1$ in canonical solar units, and $r_2 = \|\mathbf{r}_2\|$ is the heliocentric distance.

Results. Compared with NASA's JPL Horizons:

ElementComputedNASA JPL HorizonsDifference
Semi-major axis ($a$)2.2932 AU2.3610 AU0.61%
Eccentricity ($e$)0.44360.45650.51%
Inclination ($i$)9.978°10.136°0.11%
Ascending node ($\Omega$)231.708°231.401°0.20%
Arg. of periapsis ($\omega$)46.348°47.143°2.35%
Mean anomaly ($M$)1.502°1.287°17.83%

Takeaway. The formal Monte Carlo measurement spread ($\pm 0.005\text{ AU}$) is much smaller than these gaps, showing that the errors are systematic rather than noise. Two physical factors explain this: the Gauss model assumes an unperturbed two-body problem, and with an orbit inclined by only $\sim 10^\circ$, the out-of-plane signal is small, amplifying element sensitivities. The main lesson is that observational precision does not equal physical accuracy.

A Weak-Form 2D Continuity Inversion for Pulsar Birth Demographics

AstrophysicsWith Tanish Kulkarni, 2026 · Paper I of the pulsar series
In one line. Only about 0.57% of pulsars are born slow (formal 95% limit 25% to 30%), which rules out the classic 1981 claim that 40% or more are.

Question. Are neutron stars born spinning fast, or are many born slow? Neutron stars should start with spin periods of tens of milliseconds, but a classic 1981 argument (Vivekanand and Narayan) concluded that 40% to 75% of pulsars enter the population at slow periods of 0.4 to 0.85 s. Settling this from data is hard: a pulsar's spin-down rate depends on its magnetic field as $\dot P \propto B^2/P$, varying by six orders of magnitude at the same period, and telescope selection hides most of the population in a way that is itself unconstrained.

Approach. Pulsars drift through the plane of period $P$ and field $B$ deterministically, so the birth rate $S$ must satisfy a continuity equation:

$$\nabla\!\cdot\!\big(\mathbf v\,f\big)+\Lambda f=S \quad \implies \quad \iint \Big[ \mathbf{v}f \cdot \nabla\phi - \Lambda f \phi + S\phi \Big] \,d(\log P)\,d(\log B) = 0$$
Where $\mathbf{v} = (\dot{P}, \dot{B})$ is the drift velocity, $f$ is population density, $\Lambda$ is the death cutoff, $S$ is the birth rate, and $\phi$ is a smooth test function absorbing all derivatives.

We solve for $S$ from the observed catalogue. To avoid differentiating noisy histograms, the equation is written in weak form: integrating by parts against smooth test functions transfers every derivative onto the test function. The unknown telescope visibility is calibrated only outside the slow-birth window of 0.40 to 0.85 s, so the result cannot be circular.

Validation. On 100 synthetic catalogues of 2,356 pulsars, the method recovered fast, Gaussian, and two-peaked birth distributions to within 0.06 dex in log period, with a resolution of 0.29 dex inside the slow window. Statistical limits were calibrated with 300 Monte Carlo runs per test.

Results. Applied to 2,356 isolated pulsars from the ATNF catalogue, the inversion finds that only 0.57% of pulsars are born slow (at most 9.03% across all field-decay rates), with formal 95% upper limits of 25% to 30%. Four independent checks that do not depend on the fitted visibility agree:

  • Supernova remnant census: at most 22%
  • Scale-height kinematic clock: drift from the Galactic plane bounds slow births to at most 17% (in $2.6\sigma$ tension with 50% slow births)
  • Volume-complete cylinders around the Sun: at most 4% to 9%
  • High-field strip conservation bound: at most 23%

The classical 40%-or-more hypothesis is excluded under every protocol. The paper also documents its resolution limit: slow-birth fractions below about 25% to 30% cannot be resolved with current catalogue noise.

The Intrinsic Equilibrium Demographic Distribution of Galactic Pulsars

AstrophysicsWith Tanish Kulkarni, 2026 · Paper II of the pulsar series (AASTeX631)
In one line. Every pulsar is born fast, yet today's Milky Way population peaks near 1.6 s, because pulsars spend about 100,000 times longer at slow spins.

Question. Paper I shows that pulsars are born fast, with the birth rate peaking at about 48 ms. So what does the real population of the Milky Way look like today? The catalogue is a heavily filtered sample: only 2,356 timed pulsars, roughly 3% of the total.

Approach. We evolve the reconstructed birth distribution forward in time under dipole braking, slow decay of the crustal magnetic field, and a "death valley" where radio emission switches off. In steady state, the density $f(\log P,\log B)$ obeys:

$$\partial_x\!\big(v_x f\big)+\partial_y\!\big(v_y f\big)+\Lambda f=S,\qquad v_x\propto B^2P^{-(n-1)}, \qquad \tau_{\rm res}\propto \frac{P^2}{B^2}$$
Because information only flows right and down in $(P, B)$ phase space, the transport equation is solved in a single sweep, matching an independent 100,000-star Fortran simulation to $W_1 = 0.028\text{ dex}$.

Why the result flips. The time a pulsar spends per decade of period grows as $\tau_{\rm res}\propto P^2/B^2$, from about 270 years at birth to about 23 million years at maturity, a factor of $10^5$. Pulsars cross the fast regime in under 0.1 Myr and then spend about 71% of their active lives between 0.5 and 3 s. The true population therefore peaks at 1.61 s with a median of 1.38 s, even though every pulsar was born fast. No slow injection is needed.

Results.

  • After applying telescope selection (beaming, luminosity, scattering), the model reproduces the observed catalogue: median 0.62 s, $W_1 = 0.037\text{ dex}$.
  • The Galaxy hosts about $(0.82\pm0.27)\times10^5$ active radio pulsars and about $1.1\times10^8$ dead radio pulsars.
  • High-latitude pulsars, which the model was not fitted to, are predicted by ballistic drift out of the disk: $W_1 = 0.031\text{ dex}$, KS test $p = 0.199$.
  • Adding slow injection makes the match worse, $W_1$ rising from 0.037 to 0.112 dex at +50%.
  • Forecasts: discovery medians shift to 0.83 s for FAST and 1.03 s for SKA1-Mid, yet ~90% of active pulsars remain unseen because of narrow beams.

The Single-Generator Theory of Cycle-Decomposed Digit-Subtraction Dynamics

Number theoryIn review at Integers journal · August 2026
In one line. A complete closed-form theory of cycles, transients, and rank for generalized Kaprekar maps in any base, proving they decompose into single-generator multipliers.

Setting. Kaprekar's routine sorts the digits of a number, subtracts, and repeats; every four-digit number then lands on 6174. I study a generalization in any base $B$: choose a permutation $\pi$ of the digit positions, and on each cycle of $\pi$ subtract the digit string from its rotation, then scatter the result back into place. For $B=10$ and $\pi=(1\,3)(2\,4)$, the number 5678 becomes 1188.

Structure. The map splits exactly into independent pieces, one per cycle, and each piece of length $t$ is, after identifying $x\sim -x$, multiplication by a single generator:

$$c = (B-1)B^{t-1} \pmod{B^t - 1}, \qquad \text{Eventual Rank} = \prod_i \frac{N_2(B, t_i)+1}{2}$$
Write $B^t-1=N_1N_2$, where every prime of $N_1$ divides $B-1$ and $\gcd(N_2,B-1)=1$. Everything reads off from $N_1$ and $N_2$.

Results.

  • Each piece has exactly $(N_2+1)/2$ periodic states. Their cycle lengths are orders of $c$ modulo divisors of $N_2$, and the least common multiple of all lengths is the order of $c$ in $(\mathbb Z/N_2)^\times/\{\pm1\}$.
  • Every state reaches a periodic one in $a_0$ or $a_0+1$ steps, where $a_0$ is the least integer with $N_1\mid c^{a_0}$.
  • Everything collapses to zero only for $t=1$, or for $t=2$ when $B+1$ is a power of two.
  • For two-cycles, the eventual rank equals $(\mathrm{odd}(B+1)+1)/2$, which is identically the integer sequence OEIS A003602.
  • Examples: for $B=14,\ t=3$ the cycle structure has period 105; for $B=6,\ t=4$, cycles of length 1, 3, and 9 coexist.

Rediscovering a Formula for π

MathematicsIn review at Axiom journal · Elementary proof
In one line. Proves $\sum 2^n / \binom{2n+1}{n+1} = 1 + \pi/2$ and a closed form for every $|a| < 4$ using an elementary Beta integral substitution, achieving 7 digits of $\pi$ in 8 terms.

Origin. While computing $\pi$ by hand from a series, an arithmetic slip produced an unexpected series that numerically equaled $1+\pi/2$:

$$\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{2^n}{\binom{2n+1}{n+1}}=1+\frac{\pi}{2}$$

Result. The paper proves this and generalizes it to every real $|a|<4$, establishing the closed form:

$$S(a)=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{a^n}{\binom{2n+1}{n+1}}=\frac{2}{4-a}+\frac{8}{(4-a)^{3/2}\sqrt a}\,\arctan\sqrt{\frac{a}{4-a}}$$
Special evaluations include $S(1)=\frac{2}{3}+\frac{4\pi}{9\sqrt3}$, $S(2)=1+\frac{\pi}{2}$, and $S(3)=2+\frac{8\pi}{3\sqrt3}$.

Proof idea. A Beta-integral identity gives $1/\binom{2n+1}{n+1}=(n+1)\int_0^1 t^n(1-t)^n\,dt$. Summing inside the integral converts the series into geometric form, leaving $\int_0^1\frac{dt}{(1-a\,t(1-t))^2}$, evaluated by completing the square. Only integration by parts and the ratio test are used. The closed form is known in hypergeometric literature as ${}_2F_1(2,1;\tfrac32;\tfrac a4)$, so the contribution is the completely elementary derivation.

Speed. The terms shrink geometrically like $(a/4)^n$, whereas the Leibniz series needs $10^k$ terms for $k$ digits. Choosing $a=2-\sqrt2$ yields seven correct digits of $\pi$ after eight terms.

Quantum Field Theory in de Sitter Space: Foundations, Challenges, and Implications for Physics

Theoretical physicsReview paper, 2026
In one line. The thermal bath an observer detects in an expanding universe at $T_{\rm dS} = H/2\pi$ arises from horizon entanglement, preserving overall unitarity.

Question. De Sitter space, the geometry of a universe with constant positive curvature, describes both inflation and today's accelerated expansion. It is also where ordinary quantum field theory breaks down: there is no global timelike Killing vector, so there is no canonical vacuum. A one-parameter family of equally valid vacua exists, and a static observer sees thermal radiation at temperature:

$$T_{\rm dS}=\frac{H}{2\pi}, \qquad -X_0^2+\sum_{i=1}^4 X_i^2 = H^{-2}, \qquad \mathcal{P}_\phi = \frac{H^2}{4\pi^2}$$
Where $H$ is the expansion rate, $T_{\rm dS}$ is the Gibbons-Hawking horizon temperature, and $\mathcal{P}_\phi$ is the primordial scalar perturbation power spectrum.

Two frameworks.

  • Direct-sum QFT: Replaces the Hilbert space with $\mathcal H_{+}\oplus\mathcal H_{-}$, one sector for each time orientation. The global state is pure, but an observer confined to one sector sees the reduced density matrix $\rho_+=\mathrm{Tr}_-|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|$, which is exactly thermal. Thermality becomes a consequence of what the observer cannot access, preserving unitarity.
  • Ambient space formalism: Embeds de Sitter space as a hyperboloid in five-dimensional flat space, so the symmetry group $\mathrm{SO}(1,4)$ acts by linear matrix multiplication. Fields become representations of this group, correlation functions depend only on one invariant, and the infrared divergence of the massless scalar traces back to an indecomposable representation.

Takeaways. The first framework explains why observers see heat, and the second explains which fields are consistent with symmetry. Together they account for the scale-invariant inflationary power spectrum seen in the cosmic microwave background. The paper closes with open problems in graviton observables and quantum stability.

From Homology to de Rham Cohomology: An Expository Journey through the Topology of Smooth Manifolds

Topology137-page exposition, 2026
In one line. A 137-page exposition culminating in de Rham's theorem: proving that analytic differential forms and topological invariants of a manifold coincide.

Question. Local geometry cannot tell a sphere from a torus, yet the two are fundamentally different. How can calculus detect global shape? The exposition follows the thread from Euler, Poincaré, and Gauss–Bonnet to a modern answer.

Path. It builds homology (simplicial complexes, chains, cycles, boundaries, and singular homology), then cohomology (cochains, functoriality, relative cohomology, Mayer–Vietoris, and cup products), then differential forms and the generalized Stokes theorem $\int_M d\omega=\int_{\partial M}\omega$.

Destination. De Rham's theorem proves that analytic invariants—closed forms modulo exact forms—equal topological ones:

$$H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)\;\cong\;H^k(M;\mathbb R) \qquad \Longleftrightarrow \qquad \int_M d\omega = \int_{\partial M} \omega$$

The paper proves it, computing explicit examples on spheres, tori, punctured Euclidean space, projective spaces, and Riemann surfaces of higher genus. The goal throughout is presenting de Rham cohomology as the natural endpoint of linking local calculus to global topology.

How mathematical serialism works

  1. Start with a generating sequence, such as the Fibonacci numbers 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, …
  2. Read each term as an interval, the musical distance between two notes, not as an absolute pitch.
  3. Reduce modulo a chosen number $m$. Mod 7 gives seven interval classes and tonal, scale-based material; mod 11 gives eleven classes and chromatic, atonal material.
  4. Apply the intervals in turn. Each result sets the distance from one note to the next. Octave equivalence applies, so an interval class can sound in any octave.
  5. Direction is free. Mod $m$, descending by $a$ is equivalent to ascending by $m-a$: in mod 7, down 3 equals up 4. A class $a$ can also be voiced up or down by $a$.
  6. Build a transposition matrix. Its rows provide related motivic material: transpositions, retrogrades (backwards), inversions (mirrored), and retrograde inversions.

The system fixes the interval relationships only. Starting pitch, octave, rhythm, harmony, instrumentation, contour, and how material is shared between the four instruments are all composer's choices.

The deliberate exception. In measure 27 the piece breaks the rule and adjusts the sequence so that the first movement can resolve in E minor. The score shows where.
